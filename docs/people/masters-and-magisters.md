# Magisters

The Magister[^magister] is "the highest-ranking faculty Associate of Wiess College" [@constitution-2026 p.3], a professor who lives beside the college with their family, advocates for it to the university, and holds a veto over Cabinet [@constitution-1993]. The 2003 O-Week book put it plainly: "You may have heard about masters at Rice, but do not let their title mislead you. At Rice, masters are professors and their families who have volunteered to be helpful, loving and supportive people at each of the residential colleges" [@wb 20040619001755 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/01-14%20intro.pdf p.7]. The first Magister, Dr. Roy Talmage, took office with the college system in 1957 [@oweek-2006 p.36]. The table below lists every Magister the sources name. It is complete from 1990 and thin before that: for 1957–1990 the corpus holds one handbook sentence and a handful of alumni memories, and the Woodson's Wiess College Records are where the rest will be found. For the Magisters alongside the Resident Associates and College Coordinators, in one table, see [The Core Team](core-team.md).

The 2024 and 2025 books add the first written job description from the Magisters themselves: "As magisters, we have four main roles": "Advising the Student Government… Navigating Academic Advising Resources… Providing Support in Emergencies… Oversight of Physical Spaces: We work closely with Housing & Dining (H&D) to ensure that the physical spaces at Wiess are well-maintained and functional" [@oweek-2024 p.29] [@oweek-2025 p.30]. The books' own definition: "The Magisters are tenured faculty members who oversee student life at each college… Living in houses adjacent to each college, Magisters are the middlemen between students and Rice administration" [@oweek-2024 p.28].

## How the Magister is chosen

The Constitution has changed the method three times.

| Version | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1993 | The Magister is appointed by the administration [@constitution-1993] |
| 2007 | "recommended by Cabinet and appointed by the administration" [@constitution-2007 p.1] |
| 2016 | "recommended by a committee led by two chairmen selected by the President, with the President serving as ex officio. The chairmen shall select no fewer than six Wiess members as members of the committee. The President shall have veto power over membership selections. The Master shall then be appointed by the administration" [@constitution-2016 p.1] |
| 2020 | Same committee, now for "The Magister" ("led by two Chairmen selected by the President") [@constitution-2020 Art. II §3] |
| c. 2025, 2026 | Same committee, with the added instruction that members "should seek to reflect the diversity of interests and identities present across Wiess" [@constitution-hate-speech Art. II §1] [@constitution-2026 p.3] |

The governance repository narrates the differences version by version [@gov-repo]. In every version the Magister and the Resident Associates sit on Cabinet without a vote and the Magister may veto any Cabinet action; from the text of c. early 2025 (repeated in 2026) Cabinet may answer a veto with "an official statement of dissent by a two-thirds vote" [@constitution-hate-speech Art. VI §5] [@constitution-2026 p.9].

## Variants & disputes

- **When the Hutchinsons started and how long they served.** The 1994 handbook, written while they were in office, says "their fifth year as Masters"—a start in 1989 or 1990 [@riceinfo-masters]. The Dean of Undergraduates' biography in the 2014 O-Week book says he "has been involved in student life at Rice since 1994, when he and his wife, Paula, became masters of Wiess College. After seven years at Wiess…" [@oweek-2014 p.57], and their 2025 Associates profile says "As magisters of Wiess for 7 years" [@wb 20250806020200 https://wiess.rice.edu/resources/associates]. The contemporary evidence is against the 2014 bio: they were Masters in 1994 already, were still Masters at the groundbreaking in October 1999 [@rhc 2017-11-17 friday-follies-cheers] and in May 2000 [@riceinfo-news-2000-lundin], and were on stage alongside their successors for the 2002 dedication plan [@wb 20011212095214 http://teamwiess.com:80/wiess_dedication.html]. Eleven years (c.1990–2001) fits the primary sources; "seven" and "1994" are later compressions.
- **Masters or Magisters.** The college's O-Week book switched from "Masters" (2016) to "Magisters" (2017) and rewrote its history to make Talmage "The first Magister" [@oweek-2016 p.19] [@oweek-2017 p.12]; the glossary's house became "Wiess Magister's House" the same year [@oweek-2017 p.15]. The 60th-anniversary website of November 2017 still said "Andrew Schaefer '94 and Laura Atkinson Schaefer '95, Masters" and "Former Master Mike Gustin" [@wb 20171108201719 http://teamwiess.com:80/60/]. The Constitution kept "Master" through the 29 March 2017 amendment; the Constitution and Bylaws "Last Amended February 12, 2020" already say "Magister", "Magisters" and "College Magister(s)" throughout, with no "Master" left, so the governing documents followed the college between March 2017 and February 2020—not, as this page and the governance repository once said, in the 2026 rewrite [@constitution-2017] [@constitution-2020] [@bylaws-2020] [@gov-repo CHANGES.md]. This site uses "Magister" for the office in every period and keeps "Master" only inside quotations and source titles.
- **Two Dr. Bakers.** A physicist, Stephen Baker, was acting Magister in 1971–72 while Lea Rudee was on sabbatical, by his own account and his daughter's [@rhc 2013-02-19 dr-baker-rocks comment by Stephen Baker, 19 Feb 2013]; another daughter places it "in the late 60s (69 maybe?)" [@rhc 2013-02-19 dr-baker-rocks comment by Hannah Baker Hitzhusen, 19 Feb 2013]. A second Dr. Baker, Stewart Baker of English, was Magister in the mid-1970s [@rhc 2016-03-07 barbara-jordan-1977 comment by Krammit, 7 Mar 2016] [@rhc 2013-02-19 dr-baker-rocks comment by Kelty Baker, 15 Jul 2020]. Kean's post opens by calling Stephen Baker "master of Wiess" without qualification [@rhc 2013-02-19 dr-baker-rocks]; his own correction ("ACTING master") is the better reading.
- **George Pharr.** Rice Magazine wrote in 2016 that "George Pharr '75, who became master of Wiess in the early 1990s, attended the first NOD in 1972" [@rice-magazine-2016-wiess-traditions]. The contemporary record does not fit: the 1994 handbook has the Hutchinsons "in their fifth year as Masters" [@riceinfo-masters], and they were still in office in 1999–2000 [@riceinfo-news-2000-lundin]. The 2017 60th-anniversary site lists "George Pharr '75" on the anniversary committee without a title, beside "Former Master Mike Gustin" [@wb 20171108201719 http://teamwiess.com:80/60/]. Either the magazine is wrong, or Pharr served before the Hutchinsons (the late 1980s) or as an acting Magister during a sabbatical. Unresolved; he is not entered in the table.
- **Talmage's dates.** The books give only "first master" [@oweek-2006 p.36]. Alumni place him in office in 1961–62 and in the late 1960s [@rhc 2011-05-06 friday-afternoon-follies-plus-a-campus-quiz comment by Dagobert Brito (Wiess '63), 7 May 2011] [@rhc 2016-08-16 moving-in-the-rain-1957 comment by Kermit Lancaster, 23 Aug 2016]; whether he served continuously from 1957 to c.1971 is not established.
- **Stale copy.** The 2014 teamwiess.com Masters page still read "The Byrds… are brand new to Wiess" three years into their term [@wb 20140627224545 http://teamwiess.com/master.html]; it is the 2011 text [@oweek-2011 p.7], and the 2014 book itself says "fourth year" [@oweek-2014 p.102].
- **Talleyboo.** One alumnus says the 1970s award "for some notable act of unusual grossness or depravity… was called the Tallyboo, named for the former master" Talmage [@rhc 2016-08-16 moving-in-the-rain-1957 comment by Kermit Lancaster, 23 Aug 2016]. Testimony, and the only etymology we have; see [Talleyboo](../traditions/retired/talleyboo.md).

## Open questions

- **1957–1990.** Who held the office between Talmage and the Hutchinsons, and in what years: Rudee, Stewart Baker and others are known only from memory. The Campaniles (1958–1990) and the Wiess College Records at the Woodson (UA 0079) would give the list [@woodson-ua0079]. Hanszen publishes a list of its Magisters on its site; Wiess has never published one. The PDR in the Commons holds "pictures of all our former Masters" [@wb 20140719005702 http://teamwiess.com/amenities.html]: photographing the captions would give a first list.
- **2021–2026.** The Schaefers were Magisters until summer 2021, and Flavio Cunha and Fabiana Santos from August 2021 [@wb 20210806225935 http://teamwiess.com/government/ateam] [@wb 20210813231021 http://teamwiess.com/government/ateam]. Neither name appears with a title or start date in any other source we hold, and the current site's Core Team page is rendered in the browser and empty in every capture [@wb 20241006035649 https://wiess.rice.edu/government/coreteam]. The O-Week books now confirm them: the 2021 book introduces "the first year that F&F will serve as Magisters", and the 2024 and 2025 books carry their letters [@oweek-2021 p.24] [@oweek-2024 p.29] [@oweek-2025 p.30]. Whether they are still in office in 2026 is not established; the 2026 book or the live page would say.
- Whether Dr. Bill's interim term in spring 1983 [@handbook-1994] covered a sabbatical or a vacancy, and who was Magister on either side of it.
- The Donato–Kalb start date (2001 or 2002) and end date (2005 or 2006), and the Gustin–Klein end date (2011): a Thresher search would fix all three.
- Mrs. Byrd's first name appears only in the 2011 book's contact list as "Alex & Jeanette Byrd" [@oweek-2011 p.97]; the books otherwise call her Mrs. Byrd.

[^magister]: The office was titled "Master" from the founding of the college system in 1957 until the college adopted "Magister" in 2017 (O-Week book 2017 [@oweek-2017 p.20]); the Constitution and Bylaws followed by 12 February 2020 [@constitution-2020] [@bylaws-2020]. Quotations keep the word their sources used.
