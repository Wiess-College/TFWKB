# Cabinet

Cabinet is Wiess's student government. The Constitution grants it legislative power (until 2016 "the legislative and executive powers"), and makes it the body that sets and administers the Rules and Bylaws. Cabinet meets on Wednesdays.

The size of cabinet has varied: from "fifteen members" (1993) to "seventeen" (2007) to "nineteen" (2013–2017) to "17 voting members" plus a separate list of non-voting ones (2020 onward) [@constitution-1993 Art. III §4] [@constitution-2007 Art. III §4] [@constitution-2013 Art. III §4] [@constitution-2020 Art. III §§12–13] [@constitution-2026 Art. V §1]. 
The core offices—President, Vice Presidents, Treasurer, Secretary, representatives, Parliamentarian and Historian—have been there throughout; what changes typically is who is elected vs appointed, who votes, and finer details of its composition.

## Voting and non-voting

The text of 12 February 2020 is the first we've seen so far to count only voting members ("17 voting members") and to follow them with a list of additional non-voting members; it is also the first in which the Secretary, Capital Improvements Liaison and Academics and Career Chair are appointed and non-voting [@constitution-2020 Art. III §§12–13]. The undated text of c. early 2025 and the 2026 text turn the two lists into separate articles. Article V and VI
Non-voting members "may participate in debate and procedural matters, but cannot vote on matters of substance" [@constitution-2026 Art. VI §1]. The Secretary was fiormerly an elected voting officer from 1993 to 2017.

### Quorum and impeachment 
A quorum is "a simple majority (9) of the voting members of Cabinet, not including proxies" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §6]. A voting member who cannot attend "must appoint a Wiessman as proxy", and "Two unproxied absences warrant grounds for impeachment" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §8]; in 2007 it was "multiple unexcused absences", in 2016 "more than three" [@constitution-2007 Art. III §9] [@constitution-2016 Art. III §10]. The proxy rule and "Two unproxied absences" are already in the 2020 text [@constitution-2020 Art. III §7]. The 2026 text also names an Executive Cabinet made up of the President, Chief Justice, Internal and External Vice Presidents, Social Vice Presidents, Head Treasurer and Apprentice Treasurer, which "shall meet at least once per month" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §14].

## Who chooses the appointed members

Where the appointed seats come from has gone back and forth. In 1993, 2007 and 2013 "The appointed members shall be selected by the Cabinet in the spring semester", though the At-Large and Capital Improvements Representatives were individually "nominated by the President and approved by two-thirds majority vote of Cabinet" [@constitution-1993 Art. III §§3–4] [@constitution-2013 Art. III §§3–4]. The 26 January 2016 text slightly changed the general rule to "selected by the President with the approval of Cabinet" [@constitution-2016 Art. III §3]. The amendment of 29 March 2017 restored "selected by the Cabinet" [@constitution-2017 Art. III §3]. By 12 February 2020 it had swung back: "The appointed members shall be selected by the incoming President with the approval of Cabinet", each appointed office "appointed by the president with the assent of the Cabinet" [@constitution-2020 Art. III §§3, 13]. By early 2025 and in 2026 the general clause reads "selected by the incoming President in consultation with the Cabinet", and each appointed office is "appointed by the President with the assent of Cabinet" after candidates "submit an Application" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §4; Art. VI §§2, 4].

## Cabinet minutes

The Bylaws require published minutes within anywhere from one to two weeks. Very little of this survives online.
The Court's abstracts of verdicts were to be placed in the Cabinet minutes in 1993 [@constitution-1993 Art. IV §4], and to be made public by the Secretary (1993–2010) and then the College Coordinator (2016, 2026) [@constitution-1993 Bylaws Art. V §9] [@constitution-2026 Art. VIII §15].

## Representatives come and go

Beside the Cabinet stands a much larger and less consistent body of appointed representatives. The college's lists name about forty positions in 2005–06 and about twenty-five in 2023, and only ten of the 2005–06 titles survive to 2023 (College Night, Election, Food, Intramural Sports, Kitchen, Laundry, Movie, RPC, Vacuum, Webmasters): Beer Bike Captains, the Brewmaster, Study Break Reps and the "YEAH WIESS" (T-shirt screening) rep have gone; Boba, Chalkboard, Shower, Wiess Cream and UpCo reps have come [@wb 20070709183121 http://teamwiess.com/index.php?module=page&page=representatives] [@wb 20140627224604 http://teamwiess.com/representatives.html] [@wiess-rice-edu-representatives-2023]. Since c. early 2025 the Constitution has said so in terms: "There shall be an undefined number of Wiess Representatives that shall include but are not limited to" seven named positions [@constitution-hate-speech Art. VII §1] [@constitution-2026 Art. VII §1]. A year-by-year list of the known representitive positions is on [Representatives](representatives.md).
