# Cabinet

The Cabinet is the student government of Wiess College. Since at least 1993 the Constitution has placed the legislative power in it (until 2016 "the legislative and executive powers"), made it the body that sets and administers the Rules and Bylaws, and given the Magister a seat without a vote and a veto over its actions [@constitution-1993 Art. III §§1–2, 8] [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §§1–2; Art. VI §5 (8)]. Its size on paper has gone from "fifteen members" (1993) to "seventeen" (2007) to "nineteen" (2013–2017) to "17 voting members" plus a longer list of non-voting ones (2026) [@constitution-1993 Art. III §4] [@constitution-2007 Art. III §4] [@constitution-2013 Art. III §4] [@constitution-2026 Art. V §1]. The core offices—President, two Vice Presidents, Social Vice Presidents, Treasurer, Secretary, class and off-campus representatives, a Parliamentarian and a Historian without votes—have been there throughout; what changed is who is elected and who is appointed, who votes, and the arrival of Diversity Chairs, Junior and Senior Representatives and an Apprentice Treasurer between 2017 and 2020. It has met on Wednesdays for as long as the record runs: "alternating Wednesdays in the outer commons" in 1998, "every other Wednesday at 10pm in the Upper Commons" in 2014, "every Wednesday in the Upper Commons or Large Classroom" in 2021 and 2023 [@riceinfo-cabinet-1998] [@oweek-2014 p.102] [@teamwiess-cabinet-2021] [@wiess-rice-edu-cabinet-2023].

Two offices outside the constitutional list show up only on the web: a Game Room Chairman, "filled by presidential appointees", in 1998 [@riceinfo-cabinet-1998], and two "SA New Student Representatives" in 2021 and 2023 [@teamwiess-cabinet-2021] [@wiess-rice-edu-cabinet-2023].

## Voting and non-voting

Until 2016 the constitutions listed the voting members in one section and then named the non-voting ones—Parliamentarian, Historian, Magister and Resident Associates, and the SA Senator in his own article [@constitution-1993 Art. III §§6, 8; Art. VI]. The 2026 text makes the distinction structural. Article V, "Voting Members of the Cabinet", lists seventeen: President, Internal and External Vice Presidents, two Social Vice Presidents, two First-Year, two Sophomore, one Junior and one Senior Representative, two Off-Campus Representatives, Treasurer, Apprentice Treasurer and two Diversity Chairs [@constitution-2026 Art. V §1]. Article VI, "Non-Voting Members of Cabinet", lists those "appointed by the President… with the assent of Cabinet" after an application—Parliamentarian, two Secretaries, Historian(s), Capital Improvements Liaison, Academics and Career Chair—and then the SA Senator, the Chief Justice as "an observing member", the Magisters and Resident Associates, and finally "Any member of Wiess College" [@constitution-2026 Art. VI]. Non-voting members "may participate in debate and procedural matters, but cannot vote on matters of substance" [@constitution-2026 Art. VI §1]. The Secretary, an elected voting officer from 1993 to 2017, is thus an appointed non-voting one in 2026; the Capital Improvements and Academics seats, which carried votes from their creation, lose them.

A quorum is "a simple majority (9) of the voting members of Cabinet, not including proxies" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §6]. A voting member who cannot attend "must appoint a Wiessman as proxy", and "Two unproxied absences warrant grounds for impeachment" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §8]; in 2007 it was "multiple unexcused absences", in 2016 "more than three" [@constitution-2007 Art. III §9] [@constitution-2016 Art. III §10]. The 2026 text also creates an Executive Cabinet—President, Chief Justice, both Vice Presidents, Social Vice Presidents, Head Treasurer and Apprentice Treasurer—that "shall meet at least once per month" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §14].

## Who chooses the appointed members

Where the appointed seats come from has gone back and forth. In 1993, 2007 and 2013 "The appointed members shall be selected by the Cabinet in the spring semester", though the At-Large and Capital Improvements Representatives were individually "nominated by the President and approved by two-thirds majority vote of Cabinet" [@constitution-1993 Art. III §§3–4] [@constitution-2013 Art. III §§3–4]. The 26 January 2016 text changed the general rule to "selected by the President with the approval of Cabinet" [@constitution-2016 Art. III §3]. The amendment of 29 March 2017 struck "President with the approval of", restoring "selected by the Cabinet"—the entire content of that version [@constitution-2017 Art. III §3]. By 2026 the general clause reads "selected by the incoming President in consultation with the Cabinet", and each appointed office is "appointed by the President with the assent of Cabinet" after candidates "submit an Application" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §4; Art. VI §§2, 4].

## Cabinet minutes as a source

The Bylaws have required published minutes since 1993—within one week then, within two weeks from 2016, and in 2026 the Secretaries must "distribute them to the College within two weeks" [@constitution-1993 Bylaws Art. II §6] [@constitution-2016 Bylaws Art. II §6] [@constitution-2026 Art. VI §4 (2)]. Very little of this survives online, and what does is comedy.

- **riceinfo.rice.edu, 1997–2001.** The site had a Cabinet Minutes page, but it only ever said "Eventually I will get my act together and put cabinet minutes here" [@riceinfo-minutes-1999]. The news page promised minutes "brought to you by our beloved 'Cabinet Secretary'… and mangled by an HTML editor that doesn't feel like dealing with huge images" [@wb 19990218084831 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/news/index.html]. None were posted; the 1999 site has rosters, not minutes.
- **teamwiess.com, 2008–2011.** A "Last Cabinet Notes" page carried the minutes of the O-Week Themed Cabinet of 28 August 2008—the budget approved with a guessed $3,500 carry-over and a "$3335" Mailbox Budget "left over from Old Wiess when we had to give deposits for mailboxes", Freshman Rep speeches, an RUPD Campus Watch pitch [@teamwiess-lastnotes-2008]. A `cabinetminutes.php` page followed in 2009–10; its 2010 capture holds the "Silly Hats Cabinet Minutes" of 9 September 2009, in which Cabinet "heartily agrees" to approve a new Capital Improvements Rep [@teamwiess-cabinetminutes-2010]. These are the minutes-as-entertainment genre that the Wiess secretary wrote for the college.
- **wiess.wordpress.com, 2011–2014.** "Team Wiess: The Official Blog of Wiess College" published Cabinet minutes as comedy ("Lumberjack Cabinet", "Fellows Auction", "Goldenrod Speed Cabinet") alongside weekly newsletters; the blog is now private, with about fifty-two URLs in the Wayback Machine [@wiess-wordpress].
- **wiess.rice.edu, 2023–.** The Cabinet page gives the roster and the meeting time, and links the budget and Constitution as Google documents; no minutes are captured [@wiess-rice-edu-cabinet-2023].

The Court's abstracts of verdicts were to be placed in the Cabinet minutes in 1993 [@constitution-1993 Art. IV §4], and to be made public by the Secretary (1993–2010) and then the College Coordinator (2016, 2026) [@constitution-1993 Bylaws Art. V §9] [@constitution-2026 Art. VIII §15]; none are captured either.

## Variants & disputes

- **How many members?** The constitutions' stated totals do not match their lists (see [How the Constitution changed](constitution-history.md#variants-disputes)); the 1998 site's "twelve elected and three appointed offices" counts offices, not seats, and includes a Game Room Chairman the Constitution never names [@riceinfo-cabinet-1998].
- **Every other Wednesday or every Wednesday?** The glossaries from 2003 to 2017 say "every other Wednesday" [@oweek-2003 p.3] [@oweek-2017 p.14]; the 1998 site says "alternating Wednesdays" [@riceinfo-cabinet-1998]; the 2021 and 2023 pages say "every Wednesday" [@teamwiess-cabinet-2021] [@wiess-rice-edu-cabinet-2023]. The 2026 Constitution requires only "at least once a month" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §7]. The cadence probably changed between 2017 and 2021; no source says when.
- **Did the riceinfo site carry minutes?** Its reading guide says "the 1999 news page has Cabinet minutes"; the captured minutes page is a placeholder and no minutes exist in any capture [@riceinfo-minutes-1999].

## Open questions

- When were the Diversity Chairs, the Junior and Senior Representatives and the Apprentice Treasurer created, and when were the Cultural Representatives and the At-Large Representative abolished? Between March 2017 and October 2020 [@constitution-2017] [@teamwiess-cabinet-2020]; the texts are missing.
- When did the Secretary become an appointed, non-voting office? Two Secretaries appear on the 2023 roster [@wiess-rice-edu-cabinet-2023]; one elected Secretary in 2021 [@teamwiess-cabinet-2021].
- Where are the minutes? The Bylaws have required them for thirty years. The Secretary's files, the listserv archive and the Notion "Wiess Database" are the places to ask; Woodson UA 0079 holds the earliest [@woodson-ua0079].
- Who were the Presidents, year by year? The web gives 1998–99, 2021–22, 2022–23 and 2023–24 [@riceinfo-cabinet-1998] [@teamwiess-cabinet-2021] [@wb 20220826233846 http://teamwiess.com/government/cabinet] [@wiess-rice-edu-cabinet-2023]; the O-Week books and the teamwiess.com cabinet pages of 2006–2011 would fill in more.
