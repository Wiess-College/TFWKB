# Governance

Wiess College governs itself under a written Constitution, which since 1993 has been accompanied by Bylaws (how the Constitution is carried out), College Rules (what the Court enforces) and, from at least 2010, Housing Rules (how rooms are allocated). The Constitution creates a Cabinet that holds the legislative power, a Court that handles discipline, the Fellows who run Orientation Week, and the committees and representatives who do most of the work. The oldest text we hold is the Rules of 1 October 1991 [@rules-1991]; the newest is the "Complete Guiding Documents" last amended 23 February 2026, a sixteen-article Constitution that absorbed what the Bylaws used to cover [@constitution-2026]. Ten dated versions survive, all recovered from archived web pages and PDFs; everything before 1991, and most of what happened between 2017 and 2026, is still missing.

This section has four pages: [How the Constitution changed](constitution-history.md) (the narrative, version to version), [Cabinet](cabinet.md) (who sits on it and how that changed), [Court](court.md) (what it is and does) and [Rules & Housing Rules](rules.md). The dated entries also feed the [Changes](../changes/index.md) chronicle.

## How amendments work now

Under the 2026 text, Article XVI, an amendment to "the Constitution, Bylaws, or Rules" may be proposed by any Cabinet member, or by any College member through an initiative petition signed by at least five percent of the College membership [@constitution-2026 Art. XVI §1]. The petition goes to the Elections Representative, who verifies the signatures within a week; the author and the Elections Representative then draft a ballot and present it to Cabinet within a week [@constitution-2026 Art. XVI §§2–3]. A constitutional amendment must pass two votes: two-thirds of Cabinet, then two-thirds of the College in a vote in which at least two-fifths of the College participates [@constitution-2026 Art. XVI §4]. It has to be announced to the College at least one week before the vote, with the old and new text side by side, and once approved it is written into the Constitution and the "Last Amended" date at the bottom is updated [@constitution-2026 Art. XVI §§5–6]. The Parliamentarian is to convene a committee "to review the effectiveness of the Constitution and Bylaws every 3 years starting in the fall of 2020" [@constitution-2026 Art. XVI §7].

Three things are worth knowing about how this evolved. The petition threshold was ten percent from 1993 until at least 2017 [@constitution-1993 Art. XII §1] [@constitution-2017 Art. XI §1]; it is five percent in 2026. The two-stage vote (two-thirds of Cabinet, then two-thirds of the College with two-fifths turnout) dates from the 2007 revision [@constitution-2007 Art. XII §5]; in 1993 a constitutional amendment needed only "at least two thirds of the College members voting," with no quorum, after a Cabinet member's proposal was "ratified by two-thirds majority of the Cabinet" [@constitution-1993 Art. XII §§1, 4]. And from 1993 to 2017 the Constitution said that after ten amendments had accumulated "the Cabinet shall supervise the rewriting of the Constitution" [@constitution-1993 Art. XII §6] [@constitution-2017 Art. XI §6]; the 2026 text drops that rule and instead writes amendments into the body of the document [@constitution-2026 Art. XVI §6]. The 2026 text names Bylaws and Rules as amendable but states a vote threshold only for constitutional amendments; the earlier texts required a simple majority of College members voting for a Rules or Bylaws change [@constitution-2013 Art. XII §5].

## The gaps

- **1957–1993.** No constitution from the first thirty-six years of the college is on the web. The Woodson Research Center's Wiess College Records (UA 0079) hold Cabinet minutes and governing documents from 1950 [@woodson-ua0079]. The 1993 text contains the rule that a rewrite follows ten amendments [@constitution-1993 Art. XII §6], which implies earlier amended constitutions existed, but it does not itself say it is the product of such a rewrite (see [Variants](constitution-history.md#variants-disputes)).
- **1993–2007.** The 1997 webmaster already called the 1993 text "extremely out of date" [@riceinfo-rules-index-1997]. teamwiess.com's 2004–05 site had a Constitution page that was not captured [@teamwiess-site].
- **2017–2026.** The 2020 and 2023 Cabinet pages link "View Constitution" and "View Bylaws" to Google Drive files that the Wayback Machine did not capture [@teamwiess-cabinet-2020] [@wiess-rice-edu-cabinet-2023]. The office lists on those pages (Diversity Chairs, Junior and Senior Representatives, Apprentice Treasurer) show that the Constitution changed substantially between 2017 and 2020 [@teamwiess-cabinet-2020].
- **Bylaws 2016–2026.** The last Bylaws text we hold is 26 January 2016 [@constitution-2016]. The 2026 document still refers to "the College Bylaws" [@constitution-2026 Art. XI §1] but publishes none.
- **The 2021–22 hate-speech text.** The Thresher reported the clause in September 2021 [@thresher-2021-09-15]; the ratified wording and the vote date are not in any capture. The 2026 Article XV is the earliest text we have [@constitution-2026 Art. XV].

## Open questions

- When was the first Constitution of Wiess College adopted, and how many times was it amended or rewritten before 1993? Woodson UA 0079 is the place to look [@woodson-ua0079].
- Were there separate Court Procedures and a Code of Conduct, as every constitution since 2007 requires the Court to establish [@constitution-2007 Art. IV §6]? None is captured.
- Do the Housing Rules and College Rules still exist as separate documents after February 2026? The 2026 Constitution refers to "the Rules" [@constitution-2026 Art. VIII §7] but the "Complete Guiding Documents" PDF contains only the Constitution [@constitution-2026].
