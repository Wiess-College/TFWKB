# How the Constitution changed

Eight texts of the Constitution of Wiess College survive, dated 1993, 2007, 2013, 2016, 2017, 2020, c. early 2025 (undated) and 2026, plus the Rules of 1991 and four sets of Bylaws. Read in order they show a twelve-article document of about three thousand words, stable in shape for a quarter of a century, that was tightened in 2007 and 2013, restructured in 2016 (membership by the Dean of Undergraduates, search committees for the Magister[^magister] and Resident Associates, the Student Association Senator folded into the Cabinet), touched by one sentence in 2017, rebuilt in its Cabinet and Court by February 2020 (when the Masters had already become Magisters), and then rewritten, by early 2025, into a sixteen-article "Complete Guiding Documents" of nearly seven thousand words in which the Bylaws' election and budget machinery has become constitutional and a Stance on Hate Speech, first announced in September 2021, stands as Article XV; the amendment of 23 February 2026 added only an Executive Cabinet and a rule for Election Representatives [@gov-repo CHANGES.md] [@constitution-2020] [@constitution-hate-speech] [@constitution-2026]. What happened before 1993, the individual amendments of 2017–20, and the texts between 2020 and 2025 are not yet recovered.

## 1991 and 1993: the oldest texts

The Rules of 1 October 1991 are a one-page code in ten numbered sections—property, room searches, noise, roofs, dress in the Commons, Freshman Waiting, guests, enforcement, rules orientation, amendment—enforced by the Court, with penalties "set annually by the Chief Justice of the Court-elect" [@rules-1991 §VIII]. They are discussed on the [Rules page](rules.md).

The Constitution of 5 August 1993 has a preamble and twelve articles: Name and Membership, Associates, The Cabinet, The Wiess College Court, The Fellows, The Student Association Senator, The Committees, Elections, The Treasury, Impeachment, Recall and Referendum, Amendments [@constitution-1993]. Membership is determined "by the Masters of the Colleges acting together" and "is dependent upon annual payment of college dues" [@constitution-1993 Art. I §2]. There are to be "at least ten resident or nonresident associates", and "The Master shall be appointed by the administration of Rice University" [@constitution-1993 Art. II §§1, 3]. The Cabinet unites "the legislative and executive powers" and is said to consist of fifteen members, whose duties are written as prose paragraphs [@constitution-1993 Art. III §§1, 4]. The Magister and Resident Associates sit without a vote, and "The Master shall have a veto power over any action of the Cabinet" [@constitution-1993 Art. III §8]. The Bylaws of the same date cover elections (a preferential ballot for single seats, plurality for multi-seat offices, "Only word-of-mouth vote soliciting shall be permitted"), Cabinet procedure, committees and college equipment ("televisions, VCRs, microwaves, Macintoshes, film projectors…"), finance, Court procedure including the witness oath, the Rules, damages, the Fellows and college meetings [@constitution-1993 Bylaws].

## 1993 → 2007

The revision of 6 February 2007 keeps the twelve articles and the twelve offices but turns the prose duties into lettered and numbered lists [@constitution-2007 Art. III §4]. The Cabinet grows from "fifteen" to "seventeen members": the Social Vice Presidency is now always two people, and there are now two Treasurers, who "run jointly in the election" [@constitution-2007 Art. III §4 D, F]. The Magister is now "recommended by Cabinet and appointed by the administration of Rice University" [@constitution-2007 Art. II §3]. The Executive Vice President becomes the "Executive [Internal] Vice President" and must "regularly attend Student Association meetings" [@constitution-2007 Art. III §4 B]. "Freshmen Representatives" becomes "Freshman Representatives"; "his" becomes "his/her" in most places [@constitution-2007 Art. III §3]. Attendance at Cabinet is made mandatory, with "multiple unexcused absences" grounds for impeachment [@constitution-2007 Art. III §9].

The Court changes more than the Cabinet. Its authority is now framed by the "Rice University Code of Student Conduct" rather than the "Code of Judicial Procedure", and its jurisdiction is over "Class II" violations, including those by students of other colleges at Wiess [@constitution-2007 Art. IV §§1, 3]. The two Assistant Justices, who in 1993 were interviewed and recommended by the sitting Court and sat without a vote, are now elected by the College in the spring, open to freshmen, and "shall have a vote in the decisions of the Court" [@constitution-2007 Art. IV §2 C]. The President becomes ex officio to the Court [@constitution-2007 Art. IV §8]. The four 1993 standing committees (Associates, Social, Off-Campus, Publicity) are replaced by two, Appropriations and Budget, both chaired by the Treasurer [@constitution-2007 Art. VII §2]. Court fines, which in 1993 "shall not exceed the limit set annually by the Court and approved by a two-thirds vote of Cabinet", are now "at the discretion of the Court" [@constitution-1993 Art. IX] [@constitution-2007 Art. IX]. The amendment process acquires its two stages—two-thirds of Cabinet, then two-thirds of the College "in which at least two-fifths of the College participates"—and a rewritten Constitution must itself be approved by two-thirds of those voting [@constitution-2007 Art. XII §§5–6].

## 2007 → 2013

The governance repository's summary calls this step "mostly textual tightening" [@gov-repo CHANGES.md]; the text shows a little more than that. A second paragraph is added to the preamble, on "democratic student self-government" [@constitution-2013 Preamble]. The Cabinet is now "nineteen members": an Academics and Career Chair is added, nominated by the President and approved by two-thirds of Cabinet [@constitution-2013 Art. III §4 K (second)]. The Treasurers now "run for a 2-year term", "be elected separately in alternating years", and serve "as the head treasurer during his/her second year, responsible for training and mentoring the apprentice treasurer" [@constitution-2013 Art. III §4 F]. The External Vice President's job is rewritten around alumni, other colleges and the surrounding community [@constitution-2013 Art. III §4 C]. A new Section 10 requires every member of Cabinet "to update and pass on all relevant documents to their successor" [@constitution-2013 Art. III §10]. And the "Freshman Representatives" of 2007 are "First-Year Representatives" in 2013—the term appears in Article III, Sections 3 and 4 J, though "freshmen service points" and "freshmen, sophomores, juniors, or seniors" survive elsewhere [@constitution-2013 Art. III §§3, 4 I–J; Art. IV §2 C]. (The repository summary places this change in 2016; see Variants.)

## 2013 → 2016

The text "Last Amended January 26th, 2016", signed by Margaret Hodges Roddy and Michael John Passalacqua, is the largest change before 2026 [@constitution-2016]. Membership is now determined "by the Dean of Undergraduates in consultation with the Masters of the Colleges acting together", and the sentence making membership "dependent upon annual payment of college dues" is gone [@constitution-2016 Art. I §2]. The "at least ten" floor on faculty associates is dropped [@constitution-2016 Art. II §1]. The Magister is to be "recommended by a committee led by two chairmen selected by the President, with the President serving as ex officio", with "no fewer than six Wiess members" and a presidential veto over the committee's membership, before appointment by the administration; a parallel committee for Resident Associates is a new Section 4 [@constitution-2016 Art. II §§3–4].

The Student Association Senator loses a dedicated article and becomes item N in the Cabinet list, still "in a non-voting capacity", so the Constitution shrinks from twelve articles to eleven [@constitution-2016 Art. III §4 N]. The Executive Vice President is renamed Internal Vice President [@constitution-2016 Art. III §4 B]. Appointed members are now "selected by the President with the approval of Cabinet" rather than by Cabinet [@constitution-2016 Art. III §3]. Members placed on probation must resign; members going abroad or graduating early must resign; the At-Large Representative must "create a monthly state of Wiess summary" [@constitution-2016 Art. III §§4 K, 5, 12]. On the Court, the two Assistant Justices become three "Deputy Justices", one seat "reserved for a member from the new student class"; the right to appeal to the Wiess Court itself disappears, leaving appeal to the Magister; the Resident Associates, not the President, are ex officio [@constitution-2016 Art. IV §§2 C, 5, 8]. The Fellows article gains "one or more Head Fellows, who shall be sophomores or juniors at the time of selection"—the first mention of Head Fellows in the Constitution, though the Bylaws had named them since 1993 [@constitution-2016 Art. V §4] [@constitution-1993 Bylaws Art. V §7]. The Appropriations and Budget committees give way to an Associates Committee and an Elections Committee [@constitution-2016 Art. VI §2]. The Treasury now begins with "an allotment from the university" where it used to begin with "members' dues" [@constitution-2016 Art. VIII]. The one-semester cooling-off period before a justice could run for Cabinet is removed [@constitution-2016 Art. VII §3].

The 2016 Bylaws modernise the equipment list (DVD players, computers, "shirt-screening equipment"), lengthen the deadline for publishing minutes from one week to two, send the Court's abstract of verdicts to the College Coordinator instead of the Secretary, require that "Three head fellows shall be selected to coordinate O-week" and that no one serve as Head Fellow twice, and add a self-expiring Article XI permitting closed "Executive Council" sessions "as long as the number of closed sessions does not ever exceed the number of open sessions" [@constitution-2016 Bylaws Arts. II–III, V, VIII, XI].

## 2017: one paragraph

The PDF `constitution3-29-2017.pdf`, captured two days after the date in its name, differs from the 2016 text in one sentence: appointed members are once more "selected by the Cabinet in the previous spring semester", the words "President with the approval of" having been struck [@constitution-2017 Art. III §3]. The "Last Amended January 26th, 2016" line was not updated [@constitution-2017]. The change restores the wording of 1993, 2007 and 2013 [@constitution-1993 Art. III §3].

## 2017 → 2020: the Cabinet and Court rebuilt

The Word files "Last Amended February 12, 2020", from the maintainer's own collection, close the gap after 2017 [@constitution-2020] [@bylaws-2020]. Both end with the names "Tim Thomas II and Drew Keefer", the President and the Chief Justice of 2019–20 [@oweek-2019 pp.22–23]. The Constitution still has eleven articles, but much of it is new.

- **Magisters.** "Master" and "Masters", used eleven times in every text from 1993 to 2017, are gone; the 2020 Constitution says "Magister" or "Magisters" eleven times, and the 2020 Bylaws speak of "the College Magister(s)" [@constitution-2017] [@constitution-2020 Arts. I–IV] [@bylaws-2020 Art. I §4].
- **Powers.** "The legislative powers shall be vested in the Cabinet, who shall function in a republican manner to effect the will of the college"; the executive powers "shall be shared between the Standing Committees and the President" [@constitution-2020 Art. III §1; Art. VI §1].
- **Cabinet.** "Nineteen members" becomes "17 voting members": President, Internal and External Vice Presidents, two Social Vice Presidents, two First-Year, two Sophomore, one Junior and one Senior Representative, two Off-Campus Representatives, a Treasurer and an Assistant Treasurer, and two Diversity Chairs who chair a Diversity Council [@constitution-2020 Art. III §12]. Gone are the elected Secretary, the two Cultural Representatives, the At-Large Representative (whose monthly "State of Wiess" summary passes to the Senior Representative) and the paired two-year Treasurers. A separate section lists non-voting members: an appointed Parliamentarian (who keeps a "User's Guide to Wiess Government" and convenes a review "every 3 years starting in the fall of 2020"), Secretary, Historian(s), Capital Improvements Liaison and Academics and Career Chair; the SA Senator; the Chief Justice "as an observing member"; and the Magisters and Resident Associates, with the Magisters' veto [@constitution-2020 Art. III §13]. Appointed members are "selected by the incoming President with the approval of Cabinet", undoing the 2017 amendment [@constitution-2020 Art. III §3]. Absences need a proxy; "Two unproxied absences warrant grounds for impeachment" [@constitution-2020 Art. III §7]. See [Cabinet](cabinet.md).
- **Court.** "The Court shall consist of eight members": seven Associate Justices elected in the spring, who choose a Chief Justice from among themselves with the Resident Associates presiding; one Deputy Justice elected from the new class in the fall; and an Ombudsman chosen by the Associates [@constitution-2020 Art. IV §2]. See [Court](court.md).
- **Committees and elections.** Eight standing committees—Associates, Representatives, Social, Diversity Council, New Student Council, Merchandise, Senior, Budget—replace the 2016 pair; Election Representatives conduct elections "by Preferential Voting" [@constitution-2020 Arts. VI §2, VII §1]. Petitions need five percent of the College, not ten [@constitution-2020 Art. XI §1]. Head Fellows may now be seniors [@constitution-2020 Art. V §4].
- **Bylaws.** The 2020 Bylaws replace word-of-mouth campaigning with a list of banned methods, add a confidential intent-to-run form screened by the Magisters with Student Judicial Programs, write out the ranked ballot, set $50 and $100 disbursement thresholds and rules for the President's Endowment, require gender-neutral Court abstracts, and give the Head Fellows (with the A-Team) the choice of O-Week teams; the self-expiring Executive Council article of 2016 is gone [@bylaws-2020 Arts. I, IV, V, VIII].

Which of these changes came in which amendment between March 2017 and February 2020 is not known. The June 2019 O-Week book already lists Diversity Chairs and a Senior Representative [@oweek-2019 pp.23–24], and the October 2020 Cabinet page, with its Treasurer and Apprentice Treasurer, Junior and Senior Representatives and Diversity Chairs, reads as a description of this text [@teamwiess-cabinet-2020].

## 2020 → c. early 2025: the Complete Guiding Documents

On 15 September 2021 the Thresher reported that "Wiess College is adding a clause banning hate speech to their college constitution", after students were reported to Wiess Court for casual use of a racial slur; President Varun Kukunoor said the clause "will have writing representation from the Wiess Cabinet, Court and A-Team" and that "one or two other colleges have clauses against hate speech, and that's what we look to emulate", and Dean of Undergraduates Bridget Gorman noted that any college definition had to be consistent with University Policy 830 [@thresher-2021-09-15]. Kukunoor appears as President on the Cabinet roster captured nine days later [@teamwiess-cabinet-2021]. The February 2020 text has no such clause [@constitution-2020].

The next text we hold is an undated PDF titled "Complete Guiding Documents of Wiess College", from the maintainer's collection [@constitution-hate-speech]. It has no "Last Amended" line, but its producer string, "Skia/PDF m134 Google Docs Renderer", dates the export to about January–March 2025 (see Variants). It already has the sixteen articles of 2026: I Name and Membership; II Residential Associates (the Magister and Resident Associate search committees, now asked to "reflect the diversity of interests and identities present across Wiess"); III Associates; IV The Cabinet; V Voting Members of the Cabinet (seventeen); VI Non-Voting Members of Cabinet; VII The Representatives; VIII The Wiess College Court; IX The Fellows; X The Committees; XI Elections; XII The Budget; XIII Impeachment; XIV Recall and Referendum; XV Stance on Hate Speech; XVI Amendments [@constitution-hate-speech]. Elections (preferential voting, campaign rules, the confidential intent-to-run form checked by the Magisters with Student Judicial Programs), budget rules (disbursements under $50 by the Treasurer or President, over $100 by Cabinet vote, the President's Endowment), the Court's hearing procedure and witness oath, the Fellows' selection and the rule that College meetings may be called by any member—all Bylaws matter in 2020—are now in the Constitution, so the merger of the Bylaws had happened by early 2025 [@constitution-hate-speech Arts. IV §3, VIII §§10–16, IX, XI, XII] [@bylaws-2020]. Against 2020, the Assistant Treasurer is the Apprentice Treasurer, there are two Secretaries, the quorum is "(9)", the Diversity Chairs also "Advocate for underrepresented groups", and "In the event that the Magisters veto any action of Cabinet, Cabinet can issue an official statement of dissent by a two-thirds vote" [@constitution-hate-speech Arts. IV §6; V §1 (11–12); VI §4 (2), §5 (8)]. A new Article VII lists Housing, Election, Merchandise, College Night, Webmaster, Chalkboard and Birthday Representatives [@constitution-hate-speech Art. VII §1]. The Chief Justice is elected by the College and the Associate Justices drop to six [@constitution-hate-speech Art. VIII §2]. Three Head Fellows are chosen by the previous year's Head Fellow team, and Head Fellows are mentioned fourteen times [@constitution-hate-speech Art. IX §4].

Article XV, Stance on Hate Speech, defines hate speech "In accordance with Rice University Policy 830" as "any act of harassment that incites imminent criminal activity or consists of specific threats of violence targeted against a person because of that person's membership to a protected class", makes violations "enforceable by the Wiess College Court, regardless of intent", requires proceedings "in partnership with the Diversity Facilitators", allows "determinations of restorative justice", and warns that bad-faith reports are subject to Student Judicial Programs [@constitution-hate-speech Art. XV §§2–7]. Its reference to Policy 830 matches the Dean's condition reported in 2021 [@thresher-2021-09-15]. Whether this is the wording adopted in 2021–22, or a later revision of it, cannot be told from the text.

## c. early 2025 → 2026: two additions

The document captured from wiess.rice.edu on 12 March 2026, "Last Amended: February 23, 2026", is the c. 2025 text word for word with three additions: Article IV Section 14, an Executive Cabinet of the President, Chief Justice, both Vice Presidents, the Social Vice Presidents, "Head Treasurer" and Apprentice Treasurer, meeting at least monthly; Article XI Section 14, which bars Election Representatives from running in an election they are conducting; and the "Last Amended" line itself [@constitution-2026 Arts. IV §14, XI §14] [@constitution-hate-speech]. The "Head Treasurer" of the new section is a title used nowhere else in the text, which still says "Treasurer" [@constitution-2026 Art. V §1 (10)]. Everything usually described as new in 2026—the Magisters, the absorbed Bylaws, the hate-speech article, the Representatives, the elected Chief Justice—was already in place a year earlier, and the Magister wording six years earlier.

## Variants & disputes

- **Is the 1993 text a rewrite after ten amendments?** The governance repository's notes say the 1993 Constitution "already calls itself a rewrite ('the rewriting of the Constitution… before the accumulation of ten amendments')" [@gov-repo CHANGES.md]. The quoted words are from Article XII, Section 6, which is a *rule* about future rewrites, not a statement about the 1993 text's own origin [@constitution-1993 Art. XII §6]. The rule implies that an amendment-accumulating constitution existed before 1993, but whether the 1993 text replaced a document carrying ten amendments, or was an earlier voluntary rewrite, cannot be shown from the text. Treat "1993 was a rewrite" as a reasonable inference, not a sourced fact.
- **Dating the 2010 Bylaws.** The PDF carries no date; its text is the 1993 Bylaws with trivial differences, and it was first captured on 26 August 2010 beside a Constitution dated 2007 [@bylaws-2010] [@constitution-2007]. The repository tags it `bylaws-2010` from the capture date. It may equally be a 1993 text that was never amended until 2016.
- **Dating the 2017 amendment.** The only date is the filename `constitution3-29-2017.pdf`, captured 31 March 2017; the document's own "Last Amended" line still reads 26 January 2016 [@constitution-2017]. The repository dates the version 29 March 2017 and says so; the vote could have been earlier.
- **Cabinet sizes do not add up.** The 1993 text says "fifteen members" but enumerates sixteen or seventeen seats (one or two Social Vice Presidents) [@constitution-1993 Art. III §4]; the 2007 text says "seventeen" and enumerates eighteen [@constitution-2007 Art. III §4]; the 2013 "nineteen" matches its list [@constitution-2013 Art. III §4]; the 2016 "nineteen" list has twenty entries if the non-voting SA Senator is counted [@constitution-2016 Art. III §4]. The 1998 website said "twelve elected and three appointed offices" [@riceinfo-cabinet-1998]. The "17 voting members" of 2020, c. 2025 and 2026 match their lists exactly [@constitution-2020 Art. III §12] [@constitution-2026 Art. V §1]. The headline numbers should be quoted as what the documents say, not as counts.
- **When did "First-Year" replace "Freshman"?** The repository summary dates it to 2016 [@gov-repo CHANGES.md]; the 2013 PDF already reads "First-Year Representatives" [@constitution-2013 Art. III §§3, 4 J]. The 2013 date is the stronger one, being the text itself.
- **When do Head Fellows first appear?** In the Constitution, 2016 [@constitution-2016 Art. V §4]. In the Bylaws, 1993, where "Head Fellows" close the order of succession for sitting on the Court [@constitution-1993 Bylaws Art. V §7]. In the O-Week glossary, 2010 [@oweek-2010 p.90].
- **When did the Magister wording arrive?** Earlier notes on this site and in the governance repository said "Magisters" first appears in the 2026 text and that "Masters" survived in every version until then [@gov-repo CHANGES.md]. The 12 February 2020 Constitution and Bylaws already say "Magister", "Magisters" and "College Magister(s)" throughout, with no "Master" left [@constitution-2020] [@bylaws-2020]; the 2017 text still says "Master" [@constitution-2017]. The change in the governing documents therefore lies between March 2017 and February 2020, and the June 2019 O-Week book already calls the Schaefers "Magisters" in their fourth year [@oweek-2019]. The repository's CHANGES.md has been corrected.
- **Dating the undated "Complete Guiding Documents".** The maintainer's PDF "Wiess ConstitutionND.pdf" has no "Last Amended" line and no date in the text [@constitution-hate-speech]. Three readings were possible: (a) the 2021–22 text that first carried the hate-speech clause; (b) a later 2022–25 version; (c) the 2026 text. The comparison settles most of it. It is not (c): it lacks the 2026 Executive Cabinet section, the Election Representatives section and the "Last Amended: February 23, 2026" line, and is otherwise identical word for word, including the same missing committee numbers and the same review clause [@constitution-2026]. It is not demonstrably (a): it has the full 2026 apparatus (sixteen articles, Bylaws absorbed, Representatives article, elected Chief Justice), and the PDF's producer string, "Skia/PDF m134 Google Docs Renderer", is a Chrome-134-era renderer; the 2026 PDF, made between 23 February and 12 March 2026, says m147, and the 2017 Google exports captured on 3 March 2017 say m58 [@constitution-2026] [@constitution-2016]. Counting back thirteen four-week release cycles from m147 puts m134 at about January–March 2025. So it is (b): the text in force about early 2025, immediately before the February 2026 amendment. Confidence is high that it postdates 2020 and predates 23 February 2026, and medium on the early-2025 export date; how long the text had stood unchanged before it was exported, and so whether Article XV is word for word the 2021–22 clause, is unknown. The governance repository tags it `v2025-spring` with an estimated commit date of 15 February 2025 [@gov-repo v2025-spring]. The phrase "every 3 years starting in the fall of 2020", once read as a sign of a 2019–20 revision, is already in the February 2020 text and does not date this one [@constitution-2020 Art. XI §6].
- **Did the Bylaws cease to exist in 2026?** The repository first treated the 2026 "Complete Guiding Documents" as the moment the Constitution absorbed the Bylaws [@gov-repo v2026-02-23]; the undated text of c. early 2025 already has the same sixteen-article merged form, so the merger happened between February 2020 (the last separate Bylaws) and early 2025, and the repository now removes `bylaws.md` at `v2025-spring` [@bylaws-2020] [@constitution-hate-speech] [@gov-repo v2025-spring]. The 2026 PDF's table of contents lists only the Constitution, and elections, budget, impeachment and Court procedure are inside it [@constitution-2026]. But the same text still speaks of "the procedure set forth in the College Bylaws", of Cabinet members who must "read and understand the Constitution and the Bylaws", and of amending "the Constitution, Bylaws, or Rules" [@constitution-2026 Art. IV §9; Art. XI §1; Art. XVI §1]. Either a Bylaws document still exists unpublished, or the references are left over from the merger. Open.
- **A numbering gap in 2025 and 2026.** Article X promises "7 Standing Committees" and lists items 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7—Associates and Alumni, Diversity Council, New Student Council, Merchandise, Senior; items 2 and 3 are absent from both PDFs, not lost in extraction [@constitution-hate-speech Art. X §1] [@constitution-2026 Art. X §1]. The 2020 list of eight had Representatives and Social committees in those positions, then Diversity Council, New Student Council, Merchandise, Senior and Budget [@constitution-2020 Art. VI §2]. The Social Committee, named under the Social Vice Presidents, is probably one of them [@constitution-2026 Art. V §1 (4)].

## Open questions

- What did the pre-1993 constitutions say, and when was the first one adopted? Woodson UA 0079 [@woodson-ua0079].
- Which amendments between 1993 and 2007, and between 2007 and 2013, produced the changes above? Cabinet minutes of those years would date them; none are captured (see [Cabinet](cabinet.md#cabinet-minutes-as-a-source)).
- Which amendments between March 2017 and February 2020 rebuilt the Cabinet and Court and introduced "Magister"? The 2020 text shows only the result [@constitution-2020]; Cabinet minutes of 2017–20 would date them.
- What was the exact wording of the fall-2021 hate-speech clause, and on what date did Cabinet and the College vote? No text between February 2020 and early 2025 has been found; ask the 2021–22 Parliamentarian or Secretary; the Notion "Wiess Database" is reported to hold the Thresher clipping but not the text [@gov-repo CHANGES.md]. The Google Drive links on the 2020 and 2023 Cabinet pages are the other lead [@teamwiess-cabinet-2020].
- When were the Bylaws merged into the Constitution, and the Chief Justice made directly elected? Between February 2020 and early 2025 [@bylaws-2020] [@constitution-hate-speech].
- Who were Margaret Hodges Roddy and Michael John Passalacqua in January 2016—the officers who certified the amendment, presumably President and Parliamentarian or Secretary? The document gives names only [@constitution-2016]. The 2020 texts are signed the same way by the 2019–20 President and Chief Justice, which suggests the same pair of offices [@constitution-2020] [@oweek-2019 pp.22–23].
