# How the Constitution changed

The Constitution is the college's rulebook. Here's how it changed from 1993-onward: from a twelve-article document to a much, much larger sixteen-article one in 2026.

!!! abstract "TL;DR"
    - Eight Constitutions have been captured digitally and recorded here: 1993, 2007, 2013, 2016, 2017, 2020, 2025, and 2026. So do the 1991 Rules and four sets of Bylaws [@gov-repo CHANGES.md].
    - For 25 years it barely changed shape. Then, between 2017 and 2020, Cabinet and Court were rebuilt and "Master" became "Magister"[^magister] [@constitution-2020].
    - By about early 2025 the Bylaws had been folded in, and a Stance on Hate Speech (first announced in 2021) was Article XV [@constitution-hate-speech] [@thresher-2021-09-15].
    - The 23 February 2026 amendment added just two things: an Executive Cabinet and a rule for Election Representatives [@constitution-2026].

## 1991 and 1993

The **Rules of 1 October 1991** fit on one page: ten sections on property, noise, roofs, the Commons, Freshman Waiting, guests and penalties, enforced by the Court [@rules-1991]. They have their own page: [Rules](rules.md).

The **Constitution of 5 August 1993** has twelve articles. The Cabinet holds "the legislative and executive powers" [@constitution-1993 Art. III §1]. The Magister is "appointed by the administration" and has "a veto power over any action of the Cabinet" [@constitution-1993 Art. II §3; Art. III §8]. The Bylaws allow "Only word-of-mouth vote soliciting" in elections, and list college gear like "televisions, VCRs, microwaves, Macintoshes, film projectors…" [@constitution-1993 Bylaws].

## 2007 to 2017

The **2007** revision grows Cabinet from "fifteen" to "seventeen members", makes Cabinet attendance mandatory, and lets Cabinet recommend the Magister [@constitution-2007 Art. III §§4, 9; Art. II §3]. The Court's junior Assistant Justices get elected and get a vote [@constitution-2007 Art. IV §2 C]. Amendments now need two votes: Cabinet, then the College [@constitution-2007 Art. XII §5].

**2013** adds an Academics and Career Chair, two-year Treasurer terms, and the words "First-Year Representatives" [@constitution-2013 Art. III §§3, 4 F, 4 K]. **2016** is the big one before 2026: college membership is set "by the Dean of Undergraduates", search committees choose the Magister and RAs, and Head Fellows appear in the Constitution for the first time [@constitution-2016 Art. I §2; Art. II §§3–4; Art. V §4]. **2017** changes exactly one sentence [@constitution-2017 Art. III §3].

## 2017 to 2020

The text of 12 February 2020 shows a new Cabinet: "17 voting members", with Diversity Chairs, Junior and Senior Representatives and an Assistant Treasurer. The Secretary becomes appointed and non-voting [@constitution-2020 Art. III §§12–13]. The Court becomes "eight members" [@constitution-2020 Art. IV §2]. "Master" is gone; it's "Magister" throughout [@constitution-2020] [@bylaws-2020].

We don't know which amendment did which. We only have the before (2017) and the after (2020).

## 2021 to 2026: hate speech and the mega-document

In September 2021 the Thresher reported that Wiess was "adding a clause banning hate speech to their college constitution", after students were reported to Wiess Court for casual use of a racial slur [@thresher-2021-09-15].

The next text we have is undated, from about early 2025. It's sixteen articles long, with the Bylaws folded in, a directly elected Chief Justice, a list of Representatives, and Article XV, Stance on Hate Speech [@constitution-hate-speech]. Article XV ties hate speech to Rice Policy 830 and lets the Court act "regardless of intent", working with the Diversity Facilitators [@constitution-hate-speech Art. XV §§2–7].

The 2026 text is that one word for word, but defines an Executive Cabinet and a ban on Election Representatives running in elections they run [@constitution-2026 Arts. IV §14, XI §14].

??? info "The details: 1991 and 1993"
    **Rules, 1991.** Ten numbered sections: property, room searches, noise, roofs, dress in the Commons, Freshman Waiting, guests, enforcement, rules orientation, amendment. Penalties are "set annually by the Chief Justice of the Court-elect" [@rules-1991 §VIII].

    **Constitution, 1993.** A preamble and twelve articles: Name and Membership, Associates, The Cabinet, The Wiess College Court, The Fellows, The Student Association Senator, The Committees, Elections, The Treasury, Impeachment, Recall and Referendum, Amendments [@constitution-1993].

    - Membership is set "by the Masters of the Colleges acting together" and "is dependent upon annual payment of college dues" [@constitution-1993 Art. I §2].
    - "at least ten resident or nonresident associates"; "The Master shall be appointed by the administration of Rice University" [@constitution-1993 Art. II §§1, 3].
    - Cabinet unites "the legislative and executive powers" and has fifteen members, with duties written as prose [@constitution-1993 Art. III §§1, 4]. Magister and RAs sit without a vote; the Master has a veto [@constitution-1993 Art. III §8].

    **Bylaws, 1993.** Elections (preferential ballot for single seats, plurality for multi-seat offices, word-of-mouth campaigning only), Cabinet procedure, committees and equipment, finance, Court procedure with the witness oath, the Rules, damages, the Fellows and college meetings [@constitution-1993 Bylaws].

??? info "The details: 1993 → 2007"
    - Same twelve articles and offices; duties become lettered lists [@constitution-2007 Art. III §4].
    - Cabinet goes from "fifteen" to "seventeen": always two Social VPs, and two Treasurers who "run jointly in the election" [@constitution-2007 Art. III §4 D, F].
    - Magister "recommended by Cabinet and appointed by the administration of Rice University" [@constitution-2007 Art. II §3].
    - "Executive [Internal] Vice President", who must "regularly attend Student Association meetings" [@constitution-2007 Art. III §4 B]. "Freshmen Representatives" becomes "Freshman Representatives"; "his" mostly becomes "his/her" [@constitution-2007 Art. III §3].
    - Attendance mandatory; "multiple unexcused absences" are grounds for impeachment [@constitution-2007 Art. III §9].
    - Court: framed by the "Rice University Code of Student Conduct", not the "Code of Judicial Procedure"; jurisdiction over "Class II" violations, including other colleges' students at Wiess [@constitution-2007 Art. IV §§1, 3]. Assistant Justices are now elected in spring, open to freshmen, and "shall have a vote" [@constitution-2007 Art. IV §2 C]. The President becomes ex officio [@constitution-2007 Art. IV §8].
    - Four standing committees (Associates, Social, Off-Campus, Publicity) become two, Appropriations and Budget, both chaired by the Treasurer [@constitution-2007 Art. VII §2].
    - Fines go from a cap "set annually by the Court and approved by a two-thirds vote of Cabinet" to "at the discretion of the Court" [@constitution-1993 Art. IX] [@constitution-2007 Art. IX].
    - Amendments: two-thirds of Cabinet, then two-thirds of the College "in which at least two-fifths of the College participates"; a rewritten Constitution also needs two-thirds [@constitution-2007 Art. XII §§5–6].

??? info "The details: 2007 → 2013"
    The governance repository calls this "mostly textual tightening" [@gov-repo CHANGES.md]. There's a bit more:

    - A second preamble paragraph on "democratic student self-government" [@constitution-2013 Preamble].
    - "Nineteen members": an Academics and Career Chair, nominated by the President and approved by two-thirds of Cabinet [@constitution-2013 Art. III §4 K (second)].
    - Treasurers "run for a 2-year term", elected in alternating years, the second-year one "head treasurer… responsible for training and mentoring the apprentice treasurer" [@constitution-2013 Art. III §4 F].
    - The External VP's job is rewritten around alumni, other colleges and the neighbors [@constitution-2013 Art. III §4 C].
    - Every Cabinet member must "update and pass on all relevant documents to their successor" [@constitution-2013 Art. III §10].
    - "First-Year Representatives" replaces "Freshman Representatives", though "freshmen service points" and "freshmen, sophomores, juniors, or seniors" survive elsewhere [@constitution-2013 Art. III §§3, 4 I–J; Art. IV §2 C].

??? info "The details: 2013 → 2016"
    Signed by Margaret Hodges Roddy and Michael John Passalacqua [@constitution-2016].

    - Membership "by the Dean of Undergraduates in consultation with the Masters of the Colleges acting together"; the dues sentence is gone [@constitution-2016 Art. I §2]. The "at least ten" associates floor is dropped [@constitution-2016 Art. II §1].
    - The Magister is "recommended by a committee led by two chairmen selected by the President", with "no fewer than six Wiess members" and a presidential veto over its membership. A matching RA committee is new [@constitution-2016 Art. II §§3–4].
    - The SA Senator loses its own article and becomes item N on the Cabinet list, "in a non-voting capacity": twelve articles become eleven [@constitution-2016 Art. III §4 N].
    - Executive VP renamed Internal VP [@constitution-2016 Art. III §4 B]. Appointed members "selected by the President with the approval of Cabinet" [@constitution-2016 Art. III §3].
    - Members on probation, going abroad or graduating early must resign; the At-Large Rep must "create a monthly state of Wiess summary" [@constitution-2016 Art. III §§4 K, 5, 12].
    - Court: two Assistant Justices become three "Deputy Justices", one seat "reserved for a member from the new student class"; appeals only to the Magister; RAs, not the President, are ex officio [@constitution-2016 Art. IV §§2 C, 5, 8].
    - "one or more Head Fellows, who shall be sophomores or juniors at the time of selection": first in the Constitution, though the Bylaws had named them since 1993 [@constitution-2016 Art. V §4] [@constitution-1993 Bylaws Art. V §7].
    - Committees: an Associates Committee and an Elections Committee [@constitution-2016 Art. VI §2]. The Treasury starts with "an allotment from the university", not dues [@constitution-2016 Art. VIII]. The cooling-off before a justice runs for Cabinet is removed [@constitution-2016 Art. VII §3].
    - **Bylaws 2016:** DVD players, computers, "shirt-screening equipment"; minutes within two weeks, not one; Court abstracts to the College Coordinator; "Three head fellows shall be selected to coordinate O-week", nobody twice; a self-expiring Article XI allowing closed "Executive Council" sessions "as long as the number of closed sessions does not ever exceed the number of open sessions" [@constitution-2016 Bylaws Arts. II–III, V, VIII, XI].

??? info "The details: 2017"
    `constitution3-29-2017.pdf`, captured two days after the date in its name, changes one sentence. Appointed members are again "selected by the Cabinet in the previous spring semester"; "President with the approval of" is struck [@constitution-2017 Art. III §3]. The "Last Amended January 26th, 2016" line wasn't updated [@constitution-2017]. It restores the 1993–2013 wording [@constitution-1993 Art. III §3].

??? info "The details: 2017 → 2020"
    Word files "Last Amended February 12, 2020", ending with the names of the 2019–20 President and Chief Justice, Tim Thomas II and Drew Keefer [@constitution-2020] [@bylaws-2020] [@oweek-2019 pp.22–23]. Still eleven articles.

    - **Magisters.** "Master(s)", used eleven times in every text 1993–2017, is gone. The 2020 Constitution says "Magister(s)" eleven times; the Bylaws say "the College Magister(s)" [@constitution-2017] [@constitution-2020 Arts. I–IV] [@bylaws-2020 Art. I §4].
    - **Powers.** "The legislative powers shall be vested in the Cabinet"; executive powers "shall be shared between the Standing Committees and the President" [@constitution-2020 Art. III §1; Art. VI §1].
    - **Cabinet.** "17 voting members": President, Internal and External VPs, two Social VPs, two First-Year, two Sophomore, one Junior and one Senior Rep, two Off-Campus Reps, Treasurer, Assistant Treasurer, two Diversity Chairs who chair a Diversity Council [@constitution-2020 Art. III §12]. Gone: the elected Secretary, the Cultural Reps, the At-Large Rep (the "State of Wiess" passes to the Senior Rep) and paired two-year Treasurers. Non-voting: Parliamentarian (keeps a "User's Guide to Wiess Government"; review "every 3 years starting in the fall of 2020"), Secretary, Historian(s), Capital Improvements Liaison, Academics and Career Chair, the SA Senator, the Chief Justice "as an observing member", Magisters and RAs (with the veto) [@constitution-2020 Art. III §13]. Appointed members "selected by the incoming President with the approval of Cabinet" [@constitution-2020 Art. III §3]. "Two unproxied absences warrant grounds for impeachment" [@constitution-2020 Art. III §7]. See [Cabinet](cabinet.md).
    - **Court.** "eight members": seven Associate Justices elected in spring, who pick a Chief Justice among themselves with RAs presiding; one Deputy from the new class; an Ombudsman [@constitution-2020 Art. IV §2]. See [Court](court.md).
    - **Committees and elections.** Eight standing committees; Election Representatives run elections "by Preferential Voting" [@constitution-2020 Arts. VI §2, VII §1]. Petitions need 5%, not 10% [@constitution-2020 Art. XI §1]. Head Fellows may be seniors [@constitution-2020 Art. V §4].
    - **Bylaws.** A list of banned campaign methods, a confidential intent-to-run form screened by the Magisters with Student Judicial Programs, a written-out ranked ballot, $50 and $100 spending thresholds, the President's Endowment, gender-neutral Court abstracts, Head Fellows (with the A-Team) choosing O-Week teams; the 2016 Executive Council article is gone [@bylaws-2020 Arts. I, IV, V, VIII].

    Which amendment did what is unknown. The June 2019 O-Week book already lists Diversity Chairs and a Senior Rep [@oweek-2019 pp.23–24], and the October 2020 Cabinet page reads like this text [@teamwiess-cabinet-2020].

??? info "The details: 2020 → c. early 2025"
    **The 2021 report.** The clause followed students being reported to Wiess Court for casual use of a racial slur. President Varun Kukunoor said it "will have writing representation from the Wiess Cabinet, Court and A-Team". Dean of Undergraduates Bridget Gorman said any definition had to match University Policy 830 [@thresher-2021-09-15]. Kukunoor is President on the roster captured nine days later [@teamwiess-cabinet-2021]. The 2020 text has no such clause [@constitution-2020].

    - Elections, budget rules, Court hearings and the witness oath, Fellows' selection, and college meetings (all Bylaws matter in 2020) are now in the Constitution [@constitution-hate-speech Arts. IV §3, VIII §§10–16, IX, XI, XII] [@bylaws-2020].
    - Versus 2020: Apprentice (not Assistant) Treasurer, two Secretaries, quorum "(9)", Diversity Chairs also "Advocate for underrepresented groups", and Cabinet can "issue an official statement of dissent by a two-thirds vote" against a Magister veto [@constitution-hate-speech Arts. IV §6; V §1 (11–12); VI §4 (2), §5 (8)].
    - Article VII lists Housing, Election, Merchandise, College Night, Webmaster, Chalkboard and Birthday Representatives [@constitution-hate-speech Art. VII §1].
    - The Chief Justice is elected by the College; Associate Justices drop to six [@constitution-hate-speech Art. VIII §2]. Three Head Fellows are chosen by the previous year's Head Fellows [@constitution-hate-speech Art. IX §4].
    - **Article XV** defines hate speech "In accordance with Rice University Policy 830" as "any act of harassment that incites imminent criminal activity or consists of specific threats of violence targeted against a person because of that person's membership to a protected class". Violations are "enforceable by the Wiess College Court, regardless of intent", handled "in partnership with the Diversity Facilitators", may end in "determinations of restorative justice", and bad-faith reports go to Student Judicial Programs [@constitution-hate-speech Art. XV §§2–7]. Whether this is the 2021–22 wording can't be told.

??? info "The details: c. early 2025 → 2026"
    Captured from wiess.rice.edu on 12 March 2026: the c. 2025 text word for word, plus Article IV §14 (an Executive Cabinet of the President, Chief Justice, both VPs, Social VPs, "Head Treasurer" and Apprentice Treasurer, meeting at least monthly), Article XI §14 (Election Representatives can't run in an election they run), and the "Last Amended" line [@constitution-2026 Arts. IV §14, XI §14] [@constitution-hate-speech]. "Head Treasurer" appears nowhere else; the rest says "Treasurer" [@constitution-2026 Art. V §1 (10)].


[^magister]: Called "Master" in the governing documents through the 2017 text; "Magister" in the Constitution and Bylaws of 12 February 2020 and every later text [@constitution-2017] [@constitution-2020]. This site uses "Magister" for every period. Quotations keep their original wording.
