# Rooms and spaces of New Wiess

Beyond its suites, New Wiess has a set of shared rooms that every O-Week book since 2006 has mapped and every college website has advertised: two floor kitchens, a computer room, two classrooms, an off-campus lounge, a dance room, a music room, a movie room, Sparky's, the Commons and the Upper Commons ("UpCo") above it, and an office by the Large classroom that the maps of 2006–2014 mark as the campus police's and that is now Housing & Dining's [@oweek-2006 p.15] [@oweek-2015 p.19] [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b rooms]. The building's shared rooms sit in four vertical "stacks" between the wings, which is why the maps list them by floor number ("1: Laundry, 2: Music Room, 3: Kitchen, 4: Patio") [@oweek-2014 p.14]. The suites are mostly four-person doubles and singles; the exceptions are the 3-mans, the north-east-corner 5-mans, and the rooms above the first-floor "120" stack, which the Historian says was designed as a resident-faculty apartment and so differs from the rest of Wiess all the way up [@wb 20090827015957 http://teamwiess.com/rooms.php] [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b rooms]. The names used on this page are the ones in use in 2026, per the Historian; where the record used other names, they are given alongside. Wiess Grove, a garden of trees just south-west of the building, closes the page.

## The suites

Most of Wiess lives in four-person suites of two kinds, with a common room and a bathroom: **doubles**, "typically inhabited by freshmen and sophomores", two rooms "of about 20 ft x 10 ft", and **singles**, four rooms "approximately 10ft x 10ft… coveted by the upperclassmen" [@wb 20090827015957 http://teamwiess.com/rooms.php]. The 2015 book gives the bedrooms and common rooms of a double as "each approximately 16' x 12'" and prints a plan of one [@oweek-2015 p.20]. There are also **3-mans** ("3 singles with the normal accommodations") and **5-mans** [@wb 20090827015957 http://teamwiess.com/rooms.php].

**The 5-man suites.** "Popular suite at Wiess, which typically ends up being a hangout location for thirsty Wiessmen" (2003–2011); "Rooms in the corner of Wiess that each hold five people" (2014–17) [@oweek-2003 p.4] [@oweek-2014 p.102]. The Historian places them in the north-east corner: two doubles and a single, with corner windows and more space than other suites, "since they don't need room for a utility chase, neighbouring restroom or another alcove" [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b 5-man]. The maps put them over the classroom beside the Commons, on the second to fourth floors ("2–4: 5-Man", 2010) [@oweek-2010 p.14]; on the first floor the Large classroom takes their place [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b 5-man]. By tradition, before the Historian's time (that is, before 2023), the fourth-floor 5-man was the **President's suite** [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b 5-man]. Old Wiess had a "presidential suite" too, in "the tower", the end rooms of the three-storey central wing [@wb 19991011025731 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/tower.html]; whether the New Wiess custom continued it is not recorded.

**The "120" rooms.** The first-floor "120" rooms differ from all other rooms at Wiess. The stack was originally designed as a resident-faculty apartment, and its changed plumbing and walls dictated that every room above it differ from the rest of the building [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b 120 rooms]. No written source in the corpus mentions it. Machado and Silvetti's master plan included "an individual masters house" [@machado-silvetti-wiess], so an in-building faculty apartment would have been a separate, perhaps earlier, idea.

**Alcoves.** The suites open off the outdoor corridors, many through a recessed alcove with a bench (photograph below) [@wb 20170714224838 http://teamwiess.com/newstudents/rooms/alcove.jpg]. The Historian's account of the 5-mans assumes that other suites lose space to an alcove, a utility chase or a neighbouring restroom [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b 5-man].

## Wiess Grove

**What.** A collection of trees in a garden just south-west of Wiess, "unique to New Wiess" [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b Wiess Grove].

**First attested.** 5 October 2026, testimony only [T]. No O-Week book, college website or architect's page in the corpus uses the name. Machado and Silvetti's master plan covered "the reconfiguration of the intramural fields" [@machado-silvetti-wiess], but says nothing of a garden. Old Wiess's ground was famously treed: Delany photographed live oaks across its lawns in 2002 [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002].

    "The 5-man has 3-singles and 1 double—that being said, at new Wiess the 5-man has already gained some grand traditions." ## Variants & disputes

- **The five-man suites and the President's suite are older than New Wiess.** Old Wiess's Central Wing had "five-man" suites (225/226 and 325/326) and room 336, "the presidential suite", in 1999 [@wb 19991011025731 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/tower.html]; see [Old Wiess, wing by wing](old-wiess.md#old-wiess-wing-by-wing-1999).

- **What is in a 5-man?** The 2008 and 2009 websites: "3 singles and 1 double" [@wb 20080625214656 http://teamwiess.com/index.php?r=rooms] [@wb 20090827015957 http://teamwiess.com/rooms.php]. The Historian in 2026: "two doubles and a single" [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b 5-man]. Both make five beds. Either the suites were reconfigured after 2009, or the corner suites are not all alike (the fourth-floor one, the President's suite, may differ), or one account is mistaken. The current arrangement is the Historian's, as a resident; the 2008–09 description stands as the record of its time.
- **How big is a double?** "2x 20 by 10" (2008) against "each approximately 16' x 12'" for both bedrooms and common room (2015) [@wb 20080625214656 http://teamwiess.com/index.php?r=rooms] [@oweek-2015 p.20]. 200 against 192 square feet: rounding, probably, by different writers.
- **The RUPD office.** The maps show "Classroom & RUPD" from 2006 to 2014 and drop RUPD in 2015 [@oweek-2006 p.15] [@oweek-2015 p.19]. The Historian says the office is now Housing & Dining's [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b H&D office]. When it changed hands is not recorded; the map change suggests about 2015, but a map may simply have stopped naming it.
- **Computer Lab or Computer Room.** "Lab" on the maps and website 2006–2014, "Room" from 2015 and on the 2023 representatives list [@oweek-2014 p.14] [@oweek-2015 p.19] [@wiess-rice-edu-representatives-2023]. A change of word, not of room.
- **Which kitchen cooks?** Every source agrees that the third-floor kitchen is the real one and the second-floor kitchen the Thursday-night one, from 2009 to 2014 [@wb 20090827015957 http://teamwiess.com/rooms.php] [@oweek-2014 p.103]. The 2014 book's "Note, Third (not Second) Floor Kitchen" shows that freshmen confused them [@oweek-2014 p.46].
- **"Unique to Wiess."** The dance room (2008, 2015) and the recording studio (2008) are both called unique on campus by the college's own publicity [@wb 20080709214915 http://teamwiess.com/index.php?r=otherrooms] [@oweek-2015 p.21]. Not checked against the other colleges.

## Open questions

- When the 2FK/3FK abbreviations came into use: by 2019, when the glossary has "2FK/3FK" [@oweek-2019 p.14]; between 2017 and 2019, since the 2017 book still spells the kitchens out.
- The "120" stack: Facilities or Machado and Silvetti drawings would show whether a resident-faculty apartment was planned there, and when the plan changed. The architects' page has plans that were not readable here [@machado-silvetti-wiess].
- When the RUPD office became the H&D office, and what RUPD used it for.
- When the President's suite custom began and ended, and whether it descended from Old Wiess's "presidential suite" in the tower [@wb 19991011025731 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/tower.html].
- Wiess Grove: when it was planted, by whom, and since when it has had the name. A dated photograph or a Facilities landscaping plan would settle the first two.
- When the Small classroom was created; it first appears on the 2015 map [@oweek-2015 p.19].
