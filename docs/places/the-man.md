# Harry Carothers Wiess

The college is named for Harry Carothers Wiess (1887–1948), a Beaumont-born oilman who was one of the founding partners of Humble Oil in 1917 and a Rice trustee from 1944 until his death [@riceinfo-theman]. The first Wiess website's page on him, "The Man", is the college's own account, drawn from the Board of Trustees' 1948 tribute; this page keeps to it and to what the Rice archivist has added. The dormitory finished in 1949 was named Wiess Hall for him in 1950 [@riceinfo-history], and the Constitution still opens by naming the college "for the late Harry Carothers Wiess, whose generosity, with time, mind, and resources, greatly shaped the expansion of the University" [@constitution-2026 p.3]. He never saw the building.

## As the college described it

!!! quote "'The Man', riceinfo site (1999)"
    "Wiess certainly seems to have been an amazing man, rising from a Princeton University graduate to President of Reliance Oil in a space of only three years. Five years after this, in 1917, he became one of the founding partners of Humble Oil, along with several other names that might be familiar to students at Rice University: Walter Fondren, Jesse Jones, and William Farish." [@riceinfo-theman]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2006"
    "There was a man with a dream. Harry Carothers Wiess, famed oil tycoon and founder of Humble oil (aka Exxon, Exxon-Mobil) left Rice U. a big chunk of cash upon his death in 1948 for the building of Wiess College." [@oweek-2006 p.36]

!!! quote "Owlmanac 2016"
    "Wiess was founded as the 4th of the original four residential colleges (saving the best for last) and named after Harry Carothers Wiess, a respected and beautiful oil tycoon." [@owlmanac-2016 p.12]

!!! quote "Melissa Kean, Rice History Corner, 2020"
    "You wouldn't know it from the highly visible Wiess name on campus but he was only a Rice trustee for four years… His untimely death in August, 1948 cut that partnership short, much to the Institute's detriment… We really could have used this man." [@rhc 2020-05-20 harry-wiess-and-rices-first-strategic-plan-1945]

## Variants & disputes

- **Did his money build the building?** The O-Week books from 2006 say he "left Rice U. a big chunk of cash upon his death in 1948 for the building of Wiess College" [@oweek-2006 p.36], and 2015 makes it "for the building of Wiess Hall, the fourth residential hall on campus" [@oweek-2015 p.10]. The 1994 "Man" page says nothing of the kind: the gift it records is the 1946 pledge of share income to the Institute, and the building was "later" named for him [@riceinfo-theman]. The archivist's account of the 1950 dedication is that the dorm was designed as North Hall and renamed in his honour after his death [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949]. The 1994 page and the archivist are the stronger sources; the books have compressed a memorial naming into a bequest.
- **Trustee from 1944—how long?** "Life trustee" in 1944 [@riceinfo-theman]; "only a Rice trustee for four years" because he died in 1948 [@rhc 2020-05-20 harry-wiess-and-rices-first-strategic-plan-1945]. Not a conflict.
- **The epigraph.** "Socrates said that he was not an Athenian or a Greek, but a citizen of the world" closes the 1994 page [@riceinfo-theman]. The historian's notes identify the same line (Plutarch's) on a 1975 dedication plaque photographed at Old Wiess in 2002 [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002]; see [Old Wiess](old-wiess.md).
- **The Humble Oil founders.** The 1994 page names Fondren, Jones and Farish [@riceinfo-theman]. Other accounts of Humble's 1917 founding list more partners; this page does not go beyond its source.

## Open questions

- The 1945 address to the alumni and the Twelve-Point Program are posted as images and a PDF on Rice History Corner [@rhc 2020-05-20 harry-wiess-and-rices-first-strategic-plan-1945]; the PDF is not in the corpus.
- The date and extent of Olga Keith Wiess's gift for the central-wing expansion [@riceinfo-theman].
