# Places

Wiess has lived in two buildings and kept one courtyard's name across the move. The pages in this section cover the buildings, the ground between them, and the house the Magisters live in. The man the college is named for, Harry Carothers Wiess, is under [People](../people/the-man.md). Each page cites the college's own descriptions first—the 1972, 1994 and 1995 Freshman Handbooks as carried on the first website, the O-Week books from 2003, the architects' project pages—and then the alumni testimony on Rice History Corner.

**[Old Wiess (1949–2002)](old-wiess.md).** "The first new dormitory in thirty-two years" opened in 1949 as North Hall and was named Wiess Hall in 1950: 200 beds, outside balconies connecting every room, cross-ventilation and no air conditioning [@riceinfo-history]. Its own 1972 handbook called the style Brutalism and the plan "the best motel tradition" [@handbook-1972]. It became Wiess College in 1957, was photographed empty by Colin Delany '91 in August 2002—"too poorly constructed to be worth saving" [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002]—and was demolished after the move; no source in the corpus gives the demolition date.

**[New Wiess (2002–)](new-wiess.md).** Designed by Machado and Silvetti with Kirksey as project architect: 228 (or 230) beds round a single courtyard, single-loaded open-air corridors behind ivy screens, two dining halls and a shared servery [@machado-silvetti-wiess] [@kirksey-wiess]. Ground broken 5 October 1999 [@riceinfo-news-2000-lundin]; students in the building by fall 2002 [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.3]; dedication planned for 7 September 2002 with "both war pigs flying first thing in the morning" [@wb 20011212095214 http://teamwiess.com:80/wiess_dedication.html].

**[The Acabowl](acabowl.md).** The courtyard, in both buildings. The 1994 handbook derives the name from an "Academic Bowl" football game of the 1960s [@handbook-1994]; the 1972 handbook already spoke of "large grassy acabowls" [@handbook-1972]. Acatramp, Backabowl, Acaglider, Acagrill, Acababy: the vocabulary grew with it.

**[The terraces and the Bacabowl](terraces.md).** The names behind the Acabowl. Old Wiess had a Backabowl (or Bacabowl), its quieter second courtyard [@handbook-1994]. The New Wiess books call the shared South Servery roof the "Backaterrace" (2003) and then the "Acaterrace" (2006–17), and the fourth-floor balcony the "Bacaterrace" (2014–17) [@oweek-2003 p.3] [@oweek-2006 p.83] [@oweek-2014 p.102]. Present usage differs: the Acaterrace is a small terrace by UpCo, above the firepit; the fourth-floor balcony is **Toke**; "Bacaterrace" for the servery roof is Hanszen's word [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b terraces]. The page tables which name meant which space, and when.

**[The Commons and UpCo](commons.md).** The dining hall and the college's biggest room: NOD, Tabletop, Cabinet, debates. There is the Old Wiess Commons of 1957 with its 1970s mezzanine [@rhc 2016-03-07 barbara-jordan-1977 comment by Krammit, 7 Mar 2016], and the New Wiess Commons, "the largest commons on campus" [@oweek-2014 p.46]. The PDR beside it has the Masters' pictures. The Upper Commons ("UpCo") on its second floor is where Cabinet has met since 2003.

**[The basement and Sparky's](basement-and-sparkys.md).** The Old Wiess basement game room was named "Sparky's" for a NOD decoration of c.1980 [@handbook-1994]. New Wiess's basement is used for storage. The name returned in 2010 for a renovated fourth-floor room, Sparky's today [@oweek-2010 p.91]. The page also covers the TV room / Movie Room next door.

**[Rooms and spaces of New Wiess](rooms-and-spaces.md).** The shared rooms in the building's four stacks: 2FK and 3FK (the floor kitchens), the Computer room, the Small and Large classrooms, the OC (off-campus) lounge, the Dance, Movie and Music rooms, the Housing & Dining office that the maps of 2006–2014 mark as RUPD's, and the suites, including the north-east-corner 5-mans and the odd "120" stack [@oweek-2014 p.14] [@oweek-2015 p.19] [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05b rooms]. Also Wiess Grove.

**[Building lore](lore.md).** The horned owl in the brick of the servery stack, sending the elevator down, the Old Wiess cornerstone, "the tower", the O/C Bathroom, the legend that Old Wiess was built crooked, the banners on the Commons stairs, the Wiess Wall: customs and stories about the buildings themselves, each with its first attestation.

**[Wiess House / Wilson House](wilson-house.md).** The Magisters' house. "Wiess House, a small house attached to the college" in 1994 [@riceinfo-masters]; "Wilson House", dedicated in 2011 as a memorial to the RA Dr. Bill Wilson, "the only building anywhere at Rice named after a former master or RA" [@wiess-60th-faq-2017 p.4].

For the people who lived and worked in these places, see [People](../people/index.md); for what happened in them, [Traditions](../traditions/index.md).
