# The Acabowl

The Acabowl is the Wiess courtyard. Other colleges call theirs a "quad," "but that's just 'cause they suck" [@handbook-1994].

!!! abstract "TL;DR"
    - The Acabowl is the big Wiess courtyard, where people hang out, play games and study [@oweek-2019 p.14].
    - The name is in print by 1963, when Wiess offered "the less formal sports of Acabowl (touch football a la Kennedy)" [@thresher-1963-03-13-mel-lack].
    - When Wiess moved in 2002, the new courtyard kept the old name [@oweek-2006 p.37].
    - The "Aca-" prefix spread: Acatramp, Acahammock, Acaglider, Acagrill, even Acababy [@handbook-1994] [@oweek-2003 p.3] [@oweek-2008 part 7 p.3] [@oweek-2014 p.102].

## Where the name came from

The 1994 Freshman Handbook says it comes from "Academic Bowl," a 1960s football game "in which reportedly only 'academs' had time to take part" [@handbook-1994]. Note the "reportedly." We have not found that game in a direct 1960s source.

The word itself is older than the handbook thought. In 1963 the Wiess president already called it the "Acabowl" [@thresher-1963-03-13-mel-lack]. A 1979 Thresher history said the playing field between Wiess and Hanszen "came to be called (affectionately) 'the Acabowl'" [@thresher-1979-wiess-first-fifteen-years].

## Two courtyards, then one

Old Wiess had two courtyards. The Acabowl was "where the action was"; the Backabowl was quieter, for "sunbathing or studying" [@oweek-2006 p.36]. The Acabowl had the four-square court and hosted TG parties [@handbook-1994].

Four-square was "Wiess' traditional game." Even the 1994 glossary writer admitted, "I don't recall ever seeing anyone play this" [@handbook-1994]. The inflatable [War Pigs](../traditions/warpig.md) of the 1990s were built here too [@warpig-core-deck slide 31].

New Wiess (2002) has "an enormous courtyard, still called the Acabowl after the old main courtyard" [@oweek-2006 p.37]. The 2002 War Pig flew there, tied down [@warpig-core-deck slide 32].

## The Aca-everything family

Once you have an Acabowl, everything in it gets the prefix:

- **Acatramp**: "the purple and black trampoline majestically situated in the middle of the Acabowl" (1994) [@handbook-1994].
- **Acahammock**: in the glossary from 2003 [@oweek-2003 p.3].
- **Acaglider**: a "Giant swinging covered picnic table of glory," plus the **Acagrill**, both from 2008 alumni gifts [@oweek-2008 part 7 p.3] [@oweek-2010 p.39].
- **Acababy**: yes, a real kid "often found playing in the acabowl" (2014) [@oweek-2014 p.102].

Today it's still where people "hang out, play frisbee or football, and study here, especially on nice days" [@oweek-2019 p.14]. The 2024 and 2025 books dropped the glossary entry, but the courtyard is all over the rest of the book [@oweek-2024 p.25] [@oweek-2024 p.30].
