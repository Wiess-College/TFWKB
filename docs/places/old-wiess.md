# Old Wiess (1949–2002)

The first Wiess building opened in 1949 as the fourth Rice dormitory—"the first new dormitory in thirty-two years"—and was named Wiess Hall in 1950 for the trustee Harry Carothers Wiess, who had died in 1948 [@riceinfo-history]. It held about 200 students in two-room suites, every room opening onto an outside balcony, with cross-ventilation instead of air conditioning [@riceinfo-history] [@riceinfo-rooms]. It became Wiess College when the college system began in 1957 [@riceinfo-history]. Nobody, including the college, called it handsome: its own 1972 handbook called the style Brutalism and the plan "the best motel tradition" [@handbook-1972]. But the layout—rooms opening onto shared balconies over two courtyards, the Acabowl and the Backabowl—is what the college credits for its culture, and what students insisted the replacement keep [@riceinfo-news-2000-lundin]. Students moved to [New Wiess](new-wiess.md) in 2002; Colin Delany '91 photographed the empty building that August [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002]; it was then demolished. No source in the corpus gives the demolition date.

## The building

Wiess was "shaped like a giant W" with five wings: the Tennis Court Wing (rooms 101–110, 201–210), the Acabowl Ledge (111–124, 211–224), the three-storey Central Wing (129–136, 225–236, 325–336), the Backabowl Ledge (137–148, 237–248) and the Singles Wing (149–160, 249–260) [@wb 20001207075600 http://riceinfo.rice.edu:80/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/index.html]. Room 137 was not a room but the "O/C Bathroom" [@wb 20020615221715 http://riceinfo.rice.edu:80/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/backabowl.html]. The Acabowl itself was "home to a volleyball court, a few barbeque pits, some benches, and in general all of the outdoor social events of Wiess College" [@wb 20020116233355 http://riceinfo.rice.edu:80/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/acabowl.html]. The basement under the central wing held the laundry and the game room, Sparky's [@handbook-1994]; the outer commons had the KBG kitchen, built by George Hall in the 1970s [@handbook-1994]. The RAs lived in the college; the Magisters in [Wiess House](wilson-house.md), "a small house attached to the college" [@riceinfo-masters].

The O-Week books tell the growth story. 2003: the dormitory "originally consisted of about 100 bedrooms and a small lobby that was later known as the Outer Commons… Wiess was immediately popular because of its unique construction, new furnishings, modern facilities and the benefits of cross-ventilation, a big plus in Houston before air conditioning"; with the college system in 1957 "Wiess received its Commons as well as a huge addition, which included more room, a public basement area and a library" [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.2]. From 2006 the wording is: "only 100 rooms and a small Commons. Though extra wings, a second Commons area, and a basement were added later, the unique 'Motel 6' design with outdoor hallways and wrap-around balconies brought Wiess instant notoriety for its great social atmosphere. The additions of wings to the original design split the college into two sections, each with their own courtyard and personality: the Acabowl where the action was, and the Backabowl, where a quieter atmosphere prevailed for sunbathing or studying" [@oweek-2006 p.36]. The 1994 "Man" page says Olga Keith Wiess "eventually provid[ed] funds for the expansion of Wiess College's central wing" [@riceinfo-theman]; no source dates that expansion.

## As the college described it

!!! quote "1972 Freshman Handbook (as carried on the 1997–2000 website)"
    "In plan, the only suitable comparison is to mid-twentieth century design… Dining facilities, lounge, and office are all centrally located in the best motel tradition. In fact, the only missing elements are the usual gaudy neon signs and acres of parking lots." And yet: "a lack of pretension in the building and the low density of the spacious design allow a degree of privacy and freedom which makes it possible for the members of Wiess to genuinely enjoy life at Rice." [@handbook-1972]

!!! quote "1994 Freshman Handbook (history page)"
    "Wiess possessed functional advantages which more than made up for its rather plain exterior appearance… In addition, the proximity of the west wing to the gymnasium led to one of Wiess' oldest, though now defunct, traditions, 'Jock Row' (Wiess was never formally an athlete's dormitory)." [@riceinfo-history]

!!! quote "Colin Delany '91, 'wiess college | abandoned', August 2002"
    "I doubt anybody ever called Wiess pretty and meant it… Wiess dormitory had more in common with contemporary motel design than it did with the rest of the Rice campus… In the end, the old Wiess's basic limitations doomed it. It was too small for a growing Rice student population, too open to the outside for safety, and too poorly constructed to be worth saving… It will be a good Wiess, but it won't be OUR Wiess." [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2006"
    "Some insist that 'Old Wiess' as it's become known, was supposed to be temporary housing after the GI bill of WWII caused an influx in enrollment. Like most Rice myths, this one sounds good enough to believe, but contains no actual truth. Old Wiess was built to last, and it did, though 2002." [@oweek-2006 p.37]

!!! quote "Melissa Kean, Rice History Corner, 2012"
    "Newspaper articles about the new dorm all stressed that it was designed to fit in with the campus's Mediterranean architecture but to my admittedly untrained eyes it looks more like a total rejection of the ideas that had motivated the earlier buildings. It looks to me, honestly, like a motel." [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2015"
    "By the time the '90s rolled around, the administration realized that Old Wiess was literally sinking into the ground and becoming more structurally unsound." [@oweek-2015 p.10]

## Old Wiess, wing by wing (1999)

The first Wiess website kept a photographic tour of the building for incoming freshmen, set up by Head Fellow Chris Ruehl in 1998 and rewritten by Ray Wagner in July 1999: "As you might know already, Wiess is shaped like a giant W. Thus, each room can be thought of as being in one of the five wings that make up the W." [@wb 19990501150106 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/index.html] The wings, with the rooms the site gave for each:

| Wing | Rooms | What the 1999 site says |
|---|---|---|
| Tennis Court Wing | 101–110, 201–210 | "Wiess's luxurious Tennis Court Wing separates the Acabowl from a group of tennis courts"; 209–210 were the RA apartment (Dr. Dodds) [@wb 20011112112601 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/tennis.html] |
| Acabowl Ledge | 111–124, 211–224 | the most social ledge; room 224 had "the best buildup at Wiess" [@wb 20020116233355 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/acabowl.html] |
| Central Wing ("the tower") | 129–136, 225–236, 325–336 | the only three-storey section; 335 the computer room, 336 the **presidential suite**; 225/226 and 325/326 the **"five-man" suites**; rooms 125–128 do not exist [@wb 19991011025731 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/tower.html] |
| Bacabowl Ledge | 137–148, 237–248 | "a little bit quieter than the acabowl"; 137 is the "O/C Bathroom" [@wb 20020615221715 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/backabowl.html] |
| Singles Wing | 149–160, 249–260 | "filled mostly with seniors"; Dr. Bill lived on the second floor, last room on the right [@wb 19991011005025 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/singles.html] |

Two features of New Wiess that residents treat as its own customs were already here: five-man suites in the middle of the building, and a suite set aside for the President (see [Rooms and spaces](rooms-and-spaces.md)).

## Variants & disputes

- **Who wrote the architecture page.** The bibliography entry `riceinfo-architecture` credits the text to the webmaster David Cunningham. The page itself is headed "From 1972 Freshman Handbook" [@handbook-1972]; Cunningham posted it. That makes the "motel" comparison a 1972 student judgement that Kean (2012) and Delany (2002) reached independently.
- **Was it temporary housing?** Delany: "Built in the late 1940's, as Rice University was flooded with students taking advantage of the GI bill… Thrown together quickly, Wiess was a maintenance nightmare" [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002]. The O-Week books from 2006 call the temporary-housing story a myth: "Old Wiess was built to last" [@oweek-2006 p.37]. Kean reports that the 1949 newspapers presented it as a permanent building matched to the campus [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949]. Both can be true: a permanent building, quickly and cheaply built.
- **Why it came down.** Lundin, 2000: "plumbing, electricity, or the foundation itself" [@riceinfo-news-2000-lundin]. The books: "slowly sinking into the ground and… each year becoming more structurally unsound" [@oweek-2006 p.37]. Delany: too small, too open, "too poorly constructed to be worth saving" [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002]. Jeff Ross, reading the construction photographs: floors on uncompacted fill inside a beam structure that carried only the exterior walls, so interior and exterior moved independently [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by Jeff Ross, 6 Dec 2012]. The accounts agree on the foundation and differ only in detail; Ross's is the only engineering explanation and is testimony.
- **Capacity.** "200 spaces" in the 1994 handbook [@riceinfo-history]; "only 100 rooms" in the 2006 book [@oweek-2006 p.36]. Consistent if the rooms were doubles, which the 1995 rooms page says they were [@riceinfo-rooms].
- **The balconies.** Kean: "this sounds like trouble, especially the balconies." George Webb '88: "not troubling at all—they were a great ingredient of life at Wiess, and one that the students insisted be preserved when the college's new building was built" [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by George Webb, 5 Dec 2012]. Lundin's 2000 account confirms the students' lobbying for "single-loaded room corridors, external balconies, and a large public courtyard area" [@riceinfo-news-2000-lundin].
- **Nicknames.** "Early Motel 6" (Hanszen, early 1980s) and a plane towing "Motel Wiess: Hourly Rates Available" over a football game in the late 1980s [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by James Medford, 5 Dec 2012]; Wiess's own NOD theme "Motel Wiess. Sanitized for your protection!" the same year [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by George Webb, 5 Dec 2012]; "Motel Wiess" on the college website by 1997 [@riceinfo-associates]; "the unique 'Motel 6' design" in every O-Week book from 2006 [@oweek-2006 p.36].

## Open questions

- **Demolition date.** Delany found it "still standing, but abandoned" in August 2002 [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002]; the December 2001 dedication plan assumed the old Acabowl would exist on 7 September 2002 [@wb 20011212095214 http://teamwiess.com:80/wiess_dedication.html]; a Sid Rich builder claims the demolition [@rhc 2016-05-24 hanging-out-at-wiess comment by JD Miner, 25 May 2016]. No source gives the month. The Thresher for fall 2002 – spring 2003 at the Portal to Texas History should.
- **Move-out date.** Lundin planned a January 2002 move-in [@riceinfo-news-2000-lundin]; the 2003 book says the new building "opened in the fall of 2002" [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.3]. See [New Wiess](new-wiess.md).
- **The 1975 plaque.** Now transcribed from Delany's photograph (details, photo 6) [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002]: "WIESS COLLEGE / ESTABLISHED IN MEMORY OF / HARRY CAROTHERS WIESS (1887 – 1948) / HONORED FRIEND AND BENEFACTOR OF THE RICE INSTITUTE IN ALL ITS ASPECTS. A MAN OF FARSIGHTED VISION AND STEADFASTNESS, PENETRATING INTUITION AND INITIATIVE, FAITHFUL TRUSTEESHIP AND PHILANTHROPY. TO WHATEVER HE TURNED HIS HAND HE GAVE OF HOPE AND JOY, BEAUTY AND SPLENDOR, WISDOM AND STRENGTH. / SOCRATES SAID HE WAS NOT AN ATHENIAN OR A GREEK BUT A CITIZEN OF THE WORLD.—PLUTARCH / ANNO DOMINI 1975". The small lines are hard to read at the photograph's size; the reading of the Socrates line follows the 1994 "Man" page, which uses it [@riceinfo-theman]. It is a memorial to Wiess the man, not a dedication of a room; why 1975 is still unknown. The remodelled commons of c.1974 is the obvious occasion [@rhc 2016-03-07 barbara-jordan-1977 comment by Krammit, 7 Mar 2016]. Whether it moved to the new building, as the 1999 groundbreaking promised for "plaques", is on [Building lore](lore.md#the-cornerstone-medallions-and-plaques).
- **Architects.** A commenter names Staub and Rather, also architects of Fondren Library [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by marmer01, 5 Dec 2012]. The construction photographs themselves now confirm it: every label in the 1949 series reads "Staub & Rather, Architects" and "W. S. Bellows Construction Corp., Contractors" [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949]. That is a contemporary source; what remains open is which partner designed it, which the Woodson building files would show.
- **Was it built crooked?** A college legend says students moved the stakes before the foundations were poured. The record has nothing for it; see [Building lore](lore.md#old-wiess-built-crooked).
- **The central-wing expansion** funded by Olga Keith Wiess [@riceinfo-theman], the "extra wings, a second Commons area, and a basement… added later" [@oweek-2006 p.36], and the c.1974 commons remodel: none is dated in a primary source.
- **The 1949 Thresher.** A commenter points to the first Thresher of fall 1949 (Portal ark `metapth230815`) for the move-in before completion [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by almadenmike, 5 Dec 2012]. Not yet read.
- **A 1970s memorabilia site.** A reader sent Kean a link to "some '70s era Wiess College memorabilia" at lancasterteam.com/wiess [@rhc 2013-02-19 mixed-nuts-a-dr-baker-update-a-link-to-wiess-memorabilia-and-a-bobby-soxer-on-th]. Not in the corpus; worth a Wayback check.
