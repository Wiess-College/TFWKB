# The terraces and the Bacabowl

Wiess's "Aca-" place names move around, and the books do not agree with each other on them. In Old Wiess the **Backabowl** (also spelled Bacabowl) was the second, quieter courtyard behind the three-storey central wing, "Where most of the amateur and shy sun-bathers can be found" [@handbook-1994] [@wb 20020615221715 http://riceinfo.rice.edu:80/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/backabowl.html]. New Wiess has one courtyard, and the back-of-the-house names passed to its terraces. Machado and Silvetti's building has "a servery crowned by a large public terrace overlooking the playing fields", shared by the Wiess and Hanszen dining halls [@machado-silvetti-wiess] [@rice-facilities-first-100-years]. In print that roof terrace was the **Backaterrace** in 2003, "the terrace behind the Commons that we unfortunately have to share with Hanszen" [@oweek-2003 p.3], and the **Acaterrace** from 2006 to 2017 [@oweek-2006 p.83] [@oweek-2017 p.14]; in 2014 the books added a **Bacaterrace**, "The Fourth Floor balcony. Great spot to hang out or watch the sunset" [@oweek-2014 p.102]. In 2019 the two names swapped, and in 2021 the glossary settled on three: the **Acaterrace**, "The terrace connecting the second floor and the Upper Commons"; the **Bacaterrace**, the servery-roof terrace "that we share with Hanszen"; and the "Fourth Floor Balcony" [@oweek-2019 p.14] [@oweek-2021 p.18]. A further name, **Toke**, is in print from 2025 [@oweek-2025 p.48]. The books' usage is set out below as the history of the names.

How to read it. In print, one space, the terrace on the servery roof, was Backaterrace (2003), then Acaterrace (2006–17), then **Bacaterrace** (2019–21); a second, the fourth-floor balcony, was Bacaterrace (2014–17), then Acaterrace (2019), then plain "Fourth Floor Balcony" (2021), and in 2021 a third, the small terrace "connecting the second floor and the Upper Commons", became the Acaterrace [@oweek-2019 p.14] [@oweek-2021 p.18] [@oweek-2025 p.48]. The 2021 book is the latest to define all three. "Toke" appears in a 2025 profile without a definition [@oweek-2025 p.48]. In print, "Backabowl" and "Bacabowl" refer only to Old Wiess.

## Toke

**What.** A Wiess place name in print since 2025. A Fellow is found "hanging out at Toke for the views (sort of)" [@oweek-2025 p.48], which suggests an upper floor; no written source says which space it is.

**First attested.** In print in an O-Week book, 2025 [@oweek-2025 p.48]. In writing, July 2022: "TOKE" painted on a graffiti-covered table in an O-Week photograph on the college website (below) [@wb 20220709040142 http://teamwiess.com/images/oweek2022/wiessicles.jpg]. The photograph does not show where the table stands.

## Variants & disputes

- **Backaterrace → Acaterrace (2003 → 2006).** The definition is word-for-word the same, so the space did not change; the name did [@oweek-2003 p.3] [@oweek-2006 p.83]. No source explains the change. One reading: Old Wiess had an Acabowl and a Backabowl, New Wiess one Acabowl, and the terrace was renamed to match.
- **Bacaterrace: the fourth floor or the roof?** The fourth-floor balcony in the books of 2014–17 [@oweek-2014 p.102]; the servery roof in those of 2019 and 2021 [@oweek-2019 p.14] [@oweek-2021 p.18]. Hanszen's own website says nothing of terraces [@hanszen-traditions].
- **Is the "4: Patio" of the maps the Bacaterrace?** Probably. The maps from 2006 to 2017 put a fourth-floor "Patio" at the top of the stack with the laundry (1), music room (2) and kitchen (3) [@oweek-2006 p.15] [@oweek-2017 p.28]. The only fourth-floor outdoor space in the glossaries is the Bacaterrace [@oweek-2014 p.102]. No source puts the two names together.
- **Backabowl or Bacabowl.** "Backabowl" in 1994 and in the 2006–2017 histories [@handbook-1994] [@oweek-2006 p.36]; "Bacabowl" on the 1999 photo pages [@wb 20020615221715 http://riceinfo.rice.edu:80/projects/colleges/wiess/college/photos/backabowl.html]. The same variation runs through Backaterrace/Bacaterrace.
- **"Unfortunately".** "that we unfortunately have to share with Hanszen" (2003–2011) became "that we share with Hanszen" in 2014 [@oweek-2011 p.92] [@oweek-2014 p.102]. See [Hanszen (the rivalry)](../traditions/hanszen-rivalry.md).
- **Which terrace hosted Jazz Night?** "On the terrace" (2003, 2006) [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.9] [@oweek-2006 p.44]. The large servery-roof terrace is the likely one, but the books do not say.

- **The swaps of 2019 and 2021.** Between 2017 and 2019 the two glossary names traded definitions: Acaterrace became the fourth-floor balcony and Bacaterrace the servery-roof terrace shared with Hanszen [@oweek-2017 p.14] [@oweek-2019 p.14]. In 2021 the Acaterrace moved again, to "the terrace connecting the second floor and the Upper Commons", and the balcony lost its Aca-name [@oweek-2021 p.18]. So "Bacaterrace" for the servery roof is a Wiess usage in print in 2019 and 2021.

## Open questions

- **Toke.** Which space it names, where the word comes from, and when it was first used. The 2021 glossary still calls the balcony "Fourth Floor Balcony" [@oweek-2021 p.18]; the 2025 book uses "Toke" [@oweek-2025 p.48]. The record does not explain it; the 2022 table is the earliest trace [@wb 20220709040142 http://teamwiess.com/images/oweek2022/wiessicles.jpg].
- **The Acaterrace.** When the name came to mean the small terrace by UpCo—by summer 2021, per that year's glossary [@oweek-2021 p.18]; the 2019 glossary still used it for the fourth-floor balcony [@oweek-2019 p.14].
- Was the 2009 chessboard ever built [@wb 20090903015953 http://teamwiess.com/cabinetminutes.php]?
- When the Bacaterrace was first called that. The word first appears in the 2014 glossary, and the corpus has no O-Week book for 2012 or 2013.
