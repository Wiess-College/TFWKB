# Changes

This section is a chronicle: one dated entry for each thing that changed at Wiess College — a building, a governing document, an office, a tradition started, retired or altered, a name, a website — each with the source that dates it. The tradition and governance pages tell stories; the chronicle strips them to the dated facts so that a reader can see what happened in a given year, or scan a decade in a minute, and so that every entry can be checked. It runs from the construction of Wiess Hall in 1949 to the "Complete Guiding Documents" of February 2026.

## How to read it

The chronicle is split into decade pages. Each has a short framing paragraph and a table with four columns: **Date** (ISO where known, year or `c.` where not), **Change** (one sentence, with its citation), **Kind** and **Evidence** ([P] primary and contemporary, [R] retrospective, [T] testimony — see [Evidence classes](../sources/evidence-classes.md)). **Kind** is one of:

| Kind | Meaning |
|---|---|
| building | something built, moved into, emptied, dedicated or renamed as a place |
| governance | a Constitution, Bylaws, Rules or Housing Rules text adopted or amended |
| office | a position created, abolished, renamed or changed in how it is filled |
| tradition started | first appearance of a tradition in the record |
| tradition retired | a tradition reported as ended or dying |
| tradition changed | a tradition's date, form, venue or description changed |
| name | a name or term changed (the college, a mascot, a rival, a title) |
| web | the college's websites: launches, moves, closures |

A disputed date gets one row marked "disputed" with every claimed date and a link to the page that weighs them; the chant "Team Wiess" (1974, 1975 or 1984) is the standing example. Decades with no sources get short pages that say so and point to where the sources probably are; the [1960s–1970s](1960s-1970s.md) page is mostly open questions pointing at the Woodson Research Center.

## What feeds it

- **The constitutions and rules** — every tagged version in the governance repository is a dated change: 1991, 1993, 2007, c.2010, 2012, 2013, 2016, 2017, 2026, plus the fall-2021 hate-speech clause known only from the Thresher [@gov-repo] [@thresher-2021-09-15]. See [How the Constitution changed](../governance/constitution-history.md).
- **The O-Week glossaries, 1994–2017** — twelve glossaries, diffed year to year, show entries added, dropped and reworded; when the War Pig becomes "Former Wiess mascot" (2006), when "Club 13" becomes "Baker 13" (2010), when NOD moves from "the last Friday of October" (2003) to "the last Saturday" (2010) to "in October" (2017), when Jamfest gives way to Wiess Day (2014) and when Dr. Bill's entry disappears (2015) [@oweek-2006 p.84] [@oweek-2010 p.92] [@oweek-2003 p.4] [@oweek-2010 p.91] [@oweek-2017 p.15] [@oweek-2014 p.103] [@oweek-2014 p.102]. The series is at [How we described ourselves, by year](../traditions/glossary-series.md).
- **The Thresher and the Campanile** — dated print for the War Pig's effigies and flights, the coed spring of 1983, the lost balloon of 2004 and the inflatable of 2024 [@thresher-2002-02-01] [@thresher-2004-03-26] [@thresher-2024-04-10].
- **The websites** — the 1997 riceinfo site and its 1999 ranking of living, fading and dead traditions, the May 2000 move to teamwiess.com, the Cabinet and Court rosters of 1998–2001, 2008–11, 2020–23, and the wiess.rice.edu captures from October 2014 [@riceinfo-traditions-1997] [@riceinfo-traditions-1999] [@riceinfo-homepage-2000] [@wiess-rice-edu]. See [The Wiess website through the years](../web/the-wiess-website-through-the-years.md).
- **Buildings** — the 1994 handbook's history of Wiess Hall, Colin Delany's 2002 photographs of the emptied Old Wiess, the December 2001 dedication plan for New Wiess, the 2017 FAQ's note on Wilson House [@riceinfo-history] [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002] [@teamwiess-dedication-2001] [@wiess-60th-faq-2017 p.3].

Each row cites the source that dates it; the tradition pages carry the longer discussion and the disputes.

## Greatest hits

Fourteen changes that a Wiess student should know, across all decades.

1. **1949–1950** — Wiess Hall built, "the first new dormitory in thirty-two years," and named for Harry Carothers Wiess [@riceinfo-history]. → [1940s–1950s](1940s-1950s.md)
2. **1957** — the college system: "Wiess Hall became Wiess College," with two resident associates and a student government [@riceinfo-history]. → [1940s–1950s](1940s-1950s.md)
3. **1972** — the first Night of Decadence, with "about 30 male revelers" [@rice-magazine-2016-wiess-traditions]. → [1960s–1970s](1960s-1970s.md)
4. **Spring 1983** — Wiess goes coed; the "Battle Sows" are named that fall [@thresher-2002-02-01] [@thresher-1983-09-30]. → [1980s](1980s.md)
5. **1984–1986** — the War Pig takes physical form: a chicken-wire pig at NOD 1984, the first balloon at Beer Bike 1986 [@portal metapth245573 p.27] [@maxham-pig-document]. → [1980s](1980s.md)
6. **1991–1993** — the oldest surviving Rules (1 October 1991) and Constitution and Bylaws (5 August 1993) [@rules-1991] [@constitution-1993]. → [1990s](1990s.md)
7. **1997–2001** — the college goes online: riceinfo.rice.edu in 1997, the move to teamwiess.com announced in May 2000, first content there in December 2001 [@riceinfo-traditions-1997] [@riceinfo-homepage-2000] [@teamwiess-dedication-2001]. → [1990s](1990s.md), [2000s](2000s.md)
8. **2002** — Old Wiess emptied in August; New Wiess occupied, with a dedication planned for 7 September 2002 [@edesigns-old-wiess-2002] [@teamwiess-dedication-2001]. → [2000s](2000s.md)
9. **2004–2006** — the commercial pig balloon floats away at Beer Bike 2004; by 2006 the glossary says "Former Wiess mascot" [@thresher-2004-03-26] [@oweek-2006 p.84]. → [2000s](2000s.md)
10. **2008–2014** — Wiess Day begins in spring 2008 and by 2014 has replaced Jamfest in the glossary [@teamwiess-traditions-2014] [@oweek-2014 p.103]. → [2000s](2000s.md), [2010s](2010s.md)
11. **2012** — the wooden War Pig, built by the Class of 2012; "the pig will roll" [@campanile-2012 p.172] [@oweek-2014 p.103]. → [2010s](2010s.md)
12. **2016** — the Constitution of 26 January 2016: membership by the Dean of Undergraduates, search committees for the Magister[^magister] and RAs, Head Fellows and Deputy Justices written in [@constitution-2016]. → [2010s](2010s.md)
13. **2021** — a clause banning hate speech added to the Constitution after students were reported to Wiess Court [@thresher-2021-09-15]. → [2020s](2020s.md)
14. **2026** — "Masters" become "Magisters" and the Constitution absorbs the Bylaws in the "Complete Guiding Documents" of 23 February 2026; the inflatable pig had already returned in 2024 [@constitution-2026] [@thresher-2024-04-10]. → [2020s](2020s.md)

## Open questions

- The chronicle has almost nothing between 1957 and 1983 except retrospective sources. The Wiess College Records at Woodson (UA 0079) hold Cabinet minutes from 1950 [@woodson-ua0079].
- Several "changes" are dated only by the first O-Week book that mentions them; the books of 2004, 2005, 2009 (parts 1, 3, 7), 2012 and 2013 were not captured, so a change dated "2006" or "2014" may be a year or two older [@oweek-2006] [@oweek-2014].


[^magister]: Called "Master" until 2017; this site uses "Magister" for every period. Quotations keep their original wording.
