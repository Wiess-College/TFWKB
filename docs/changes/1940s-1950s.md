# 1940s–1950s

Wiess began as a dormitory and became a college. Everything we can date in its first decade comes from one text, the history section of the 1994 Freshman Handbook as the first Wiess website carried it: Wiess Hall built in 1949, named in 1950, and turned into Wiess College in 1957 when the residential college system a student–faculty committee had recommended the year before was put into effect [@riceinfo-history]. That text is itself a retrospective written some forty years after the events, so every row below is tagged [R]. The contemporary record—Thresher reports, the committee's papers, the first Cabinet minutes—is in the Woodson Research Center, not on the web [@woodson-ua0079].

| Date | Change | Kind | Evidence |

| 1949 | Wiess Hall completed—"the first new dormitory in thirty-two years," which "completed the compass formed by the three older dorms"; 200 spaces; outside balconies, private showers between pairs of rooms, cross-ventilation and no air-conditioning [@riceinfo-history] | building | [R] |
| 1950 | The new dormitory "was named Wiess Hall in honor of Harry Carothers Wiess" [@riceinfo-history] | name | [R] |

| c. 1950s | "Jock Row": the west wing's nearness to the gymnasium produces "one of Wiess' oldest, though now defunct, traditions"—undated; "Wiess was never formally an athlete's dormitory" [@riceinfo-history] | tradition started | [R] |

## Open questions

- When was the first Wiess College Constitution adopted, and who sat on the first Cabinet? No source yet; Woodson UA 0079 [@woodson-ua0079].
- Who was the first Magister[^magister]? The handbook's Masters page starts with the Hutchinsons in their fifth year in 1994 [@riceinfo-masters]; the 1957 appointment is not in the corpus. See [Magisters](../people/masters-and-magisters.md).
- When did Jock Row end, and what exactly was it? The 1994 text says only that it is defunct [@riceinfo-history]; see [Jock Row](../traditions/retired/jock-row.md).
- The 1956 committee: who served, and what did its report say? The riceinfo text is a two-sentence summary [@riceinfo-history].
- Which Thresher issues of 1949–57 report the dormitory, its naming and the college conversion? The Portal to Texas History holds the run [@thresher-portal]; not yet searched.


[^magister]: Called "Master" until 2017; this site uses "Magister" for every period. Quotations keep their original wording.
