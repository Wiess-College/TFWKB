# NOD 2026

This is the decision record for the end of [Night of Decadence](../traditions/retired/night-of-decadence.md), Wiess's yearly public party, cancelled for good on 5 June 2024 [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].

**Status:** decided · **Date:** 2024-06-05

!!! note "Why this page is called NOD 2026"
    The decision this record describes was announced on **5 June 2024**, not in 2026 [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled]. The page keeps the name the college gave it, "NOD 2026", so that existing links hold. This record was reconstructed from the public record by a researcher, not written by the people who made the decision; the passages marked **To be supplied** are theirs to write.

!!! abstract "TL;DR"
    - On 5 June 2024 Night of Decadence was cancelled for good, by the Dean of Undergraduates on the Wiess Magisters' recommendation [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].
    - It came seven months after NOD 2023 was shut down early, with seven students taken to hospital [@thresher-2023-11-01-shutdown].
    - Wiess kept the right to throw "a 'radically different' public party of its own design". That became Masquerade After Dark, in January 2025 [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled] [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews].

The full history of the party is on [Night of Decadence](../traditions/retired/night-of-decadence.md#controversy-and-response-19982025).

## Context

**A long argument.** In 1999 ten of the sixteen college Magisters asked Wiess to "carefully consider whether it is a good idea to continue sponsoring" NOD [@thresher-1999-02-05-masters-letter]. Rice reformed the party instead of ending it: security, talks beforehand, no sexual decorations [@thresher-1999-04-23-presidents] [@thresher-1999-09-24-decorations]. After eleven transports in 2012, the alcohol policy got stricter [@thresher-2012-11-nod-transports] [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].

**NOD 2023.** Rice crisis management shut the party down nearly two hours early. Seven students went to hospital [@thresher-2023-11-01-shutdown]. On 3 November the Dean cancelled campus public parties through spring break and put NOD "on probation" [@fox26-2023-11-03].

??? info "More context"
    - **1999.** The Magisters' letter described "abusive consumption of alcohol" and "the cultural tolerance for and real risk of sexual assault" [@thresher-1999-02-05-masters-letter]. A committee of the eight college presidents and the SA President recommended security at every college, talks before the party and no sexual decorations; the Vice President for Student Affairs ordered the decorations removed for NOD 1999 [@thresher-1999-04-23-presidents] [@thresher-1999-09-24-decorations]. Rice's alcohol policy was revised alongside [@thresher-1999-04-23-alcohol-policy].
    - **2012.** After eleven transports, by the Thresher's later account, the dean "convene[d] an APAC and harden[ed] the alcohol policy" [@thresher-2012-11-nod-transports] [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].
    - **2023, in full.** The party of 28–29 October 2023: more than two dozen students treated on site, seven taken to hospital, and an altercation with RUPD that ended with three students in handcuffs. Wiess's Chief Justice said "all of Rice and Houston medical resources at NOD were becoming completely overwhelmed" [@thresher-2023-11-01-shutdown]. Dean Bridget Gorman's probation meant "reduced capacity and enhanced safety protocols at future parties" [@fox26-2023-11-03], a period "during which administration and Wiess leadership would reevaluate the party's logistics". In November she said she didn't intend to cancel the party permanently [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].
    - **The Wiess Magister's research.** During the probation a Wiess Magister studied NOD (talking with RUPD, REMS, crisis management, students and administrators, reading the research on fraternity parties and the Thresher since 1999) "to design an intervention to change NOD". The talk concluded the community "could indeed perceive NOD as a frat party with all its undesired elements: Objectification of women. Toxic male behavior. Unsafe drinking practices", called the alcohol crises "cyclical" (1999, 2012, 2023), and found "no findings about proven solution for altering perceptions and behaviors". It asked "Can I (or Admin) Truly Cancel NOD?" and answered, at the time, "No. Cultural traditions define our identity", while listing "crisis moments" and "perceived harm or inappropriateness" as reasons groups drop traditions [@nod-talk-magister].
    - **Student opinion.** Thresher opinion pieces in 2023–24 argued for treating it as public health, not discipline [@thresher-2023-11-07-public-health] [@thresher-2023-11-01-editorial], and for giving students back control of publics: "if RUPD decides who can get in, when the party shuts down … then who has the real power?" [@thresher-2024-01-30-burden-of-tradition].

*To be supplied:* the probation review itself—what Wiess leadership and the administration examined, what was proposed to save the party, and whether Cabinet discussed or voted on NOD between November 2023 and June 2024.

## Decision

Rice "permanently canceled the public". The message, signed by the Dean of Undergraduates and the Wiess Magister, said: "We have observed a disturbing, reoccurring NOD phenomenon: large numbers of hospital transports due to excessive hard alcohol consumption, flouting the University's alcohol policy, jeopardizing student safety, and straining both University and city resources" [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].

Wiess may hold a "radically different" public of its own design, but "the theme, dress code and name 'Night of Decadence' will not remain" [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].

*To be supplied:* the full text of the 5 June 2024 message, with an archived copy.

## Who decided

The **Dean of Undergraduates**, Bridget Gorman, on the recommendation of the **Wiess Magisters**: "The conclusion that we should permanently cancel NOD was a recommendation made to me by the Wiess College magisters, and I support that decision." The Dean and the Wiess Magister (the Thresher names Flavio Cunha) announced it together [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].

The sources we have don't mention a vote of Cabinet or the College. Wiess's Social VPs later said the new party's format was "entirely [administration] and our magister's decision, and we had no say in it" [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews].

*To be supplied:* whether Cabinet was consulted, and how the Magisters reached their recommendation.

## Consequences

- **A new public.** Wiess students voted on a theme from 25 June 2024. "Masquerade After Dark" (MAD) was announced on 18 October 2024 and held on 25 January 2025: outdoors, semi-formal, in January. The administration asked that the ads "emphasize that clothes are required" [@thresher-2024-10-mad-announced] [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews]. The Thresher's headline: "met with mixed reviews" [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews].
- **Money.** NOD had cost Wiess $10,000 of a $49,433 budget in 2023 [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled]. We haven't found where that money goes now.
- **Campus alcohol policy.** After Dis-O in August 2025, the Dean told the college presidents a campus alcohol ban had been "seriously considered". The Thresher's editors wrote that "another night like Dis-O, and Rice could become a dry campus" [@thresher-2025-09-03-texas-party] [@thresher-2025-08-27-culture-of-care].
- **The Constitution.** The February 2026 text doesn't mention NOD. The Social VPs "Plan and direct the social activities of the College including the public parties" [@constitution-2026 Art. V §1 (4)].

??? info "More on the consequences"
    - The Social VPs on MAD: "We, [along with] our magisters, have made it very clear we want to start to steer away from NOD's history" [@thresher-2024-10-mad-announced] [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews].
    - Four times the intoxication transports of the three previous years at Dis-O 2025 [@thresher-2025-09-03-texas-party]. The editorial also said NOD, "the largest party of the year … is essentially lost for new students" [@thresher-2025-08-27-culture-of-care].

## What was preserved

As far as the sources say: **a Wiess public**, which the announcement kept on purpose [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled], and **an annual theme**. MAD's officers planned to pick one each year, "much like how NOD had annual themes" [@thresher-2024-10-mad-announced]. The name, the dress code, the Halloween date and the Commons were not kept [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled] [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews].
