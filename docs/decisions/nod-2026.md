# NOD 2026

**Status:** decided · **Date:** 2024-06-05

!!! note "Why this page is called NOD 2026"
    The decision this record describes was announced on **5 June 2024**, not in 2026 [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled]. The page keeps the name the college gave it, "NOD 2026", so that existing links hold. This record was reconstructed from the public record by a researcher, not written by the people who made the decision; the passages marked **To be supplied** are theirs to write.

Night of Decadence was cancelled permanently by the Dean of Undergraduates on the recommendation of the Wiess Magisters, seven months after the fiftieth-anniversary party was shut down early with seven students in hospital. The history of the party and of the controversy that preceded the decision is on [Night of Decadence](../traditions/night-of-decadence.md#controversy-and-response-19982025).

## Context

**Twenty-five years of argument.** The decision had a long background. In January 1999 ten of the sixteen college Magisters asked Wiess to "carefully consider whether it is a good idea to continue sponsoring" NOD, describing "abusive consumption of alcohol" and "the cultural tolerance for and real risk of sexual assault" [@thresher-1999-02-05-masters-letter]. The university's answer was to reform the party, not end it: a committee of the eight college presidents and the SA President recommended security at every college, talks before the party and no sexual decorations, and the Vice President for Student Affairs ordered the decorations removed for NOD 1999 [@thresher-1999-04-23-presidents] [@thresher-1999-09-24-decorations]. Rice's alcohol policy was revised alongside it [@thresher-1999-04-23-alcohol-policy]. Eleven students were transported from NOD 2012, after which, by the Thresher's later account, the dean "convene[d] an APAC and harden[ed] the alcohol policy" [@thresher-2012-11-nod-transports] [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].

**NOD 2023.** The party of 28–29 October 2023 was shut down nearly two hours early by Rice crisis management: more than two dozen students were treated on site and seven taken to hospital, and an altercation with RUPD ended with three students in handcuffs. Wiess's Chief Justice said "all of Rice and Houston medical resources at NOD were becoming completely overwhelmed" [@thresher-2023-11-01-shutdown]. On 3 November the Dean of Undergraduates, Bridget Gorman, cancelled campus-wide public parties through spring break and placed NOD "on probation, with reduced capacity and enhanced safety protocols at future parties" [@fox26-2023-11-03]; the probation was, in the Thresher's words, a period "during which administration and Wiess leadership would reevaluate the party's logistics" [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled]. Gorman said in November that she did not intend to cancel the party permanently [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].

**The Wiess Magister's research.** During the probation a Wiess Magister studied NOD — through conversations with RUPD, REMS and crisis management, current and former students and administrators, the academic literature on fraternity parties, and the Thresher's coverage since 1999 — "to understand what probation meant" and "to design an intervention to change NOD" [@nod-talk-magister]. The talk concluded that the Rice community "could indeed perceive NOD as a frat party with all its undesired elements: Objectification of women. Toxic male behavior. Unsafe drinking practices", noted that the alcohol crises were "cyclical" (1999, 2012, 2023), and found in the literature "no findings about proven solution for altering perceptions and behaviors". It asked "Can I (or Admin) Truly Cancel NOD?" and answered, at the time, "No. Cultural traditions define our identity", while listing "crisis moments" and "perceived harm or inappropriateness" among the reasons groups abandon traditions [@nod-talk-magister].

**Student opinion.** The Thresher's opinion pages in 2023–24 argued both for treating the problem as public health rather than discipline [@thresher-2023-11-07-public-health] [@thresher-2023-11-01-editorial] and for returning control of publics to students: "if RUPD decides who can get in, when the party shuts down … then who has the real power?" [@thresher-2024-01-30-horton].

## Decision

As announced on 5 June 2024: Rice "permanently canceled the public". The message, signed by the Dean of Undergraduates and the Wiess Magister, gave its reasons: "We have observed a disturbing, reoccurring NOD phenomenon: large numbers of hospital transports due to excessive hard alcohol consumption, flouting the University's alcohol policy, jeopardizing student safety, and straining both University and city resources", and "students rely on alcohol to mitigate their discomfort around attending this event". Wiess may hold a "radically different" public of its own design, but "the theme, dress code and name 'Night of Decadence' will not remain" [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled].

## Who decided

The **Dean of Undergraduates**, Bridget Gorman, on the recommendation of the **Wiess Magisters**: "The conclusion that we should permanently cancel NOD was a recommendation made to me by the Wiess College magisters, and I support that decision." The announcement was made jointly by the Dean and the Wiess Magister (the Thresher names Flavio Cunha) [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled]. No source records a vote of the Wiess Cabinet or of the College; Wiess's Social Vice Presidents later said the format of the successor party was "entirely [administration] and our magister's decision, and we had no say in it" [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews].

## Consequences

- **Wiess's public.** Wiess students voted on a new theme from 25 June 2024; "Masquerade After Dark" (MAD) was announced at FITQ on 18 October 2024 and held on 25 January 2025 — outdoors, semi-formal, in January rather than at Halloween, with the administration asking that the advertising "emphasize that clothes are required". The Social Vice Presidents: "We, [along with] our magisters, have made it very clear we want to start to steer away from NOD's history" [@thresher-2024-10-mad-announced] [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews]. The Thresher's report of the party was headed "met with mixed reviews" [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews].
- **Money.** NOD had cost Wiess $10,000 of a $49,433 budget in 2023 [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled]. How the money is now spent is not recorded.
- **Campus alcohol policy.** The threat did not end with NOD. After Dis-O in August 2025, with four times the intoxication transports of the three previous years, the Dean told the college presidents that a campus alcohol ban had been "seriously considered"; the Thresher's editorial board wrote that "another night like Dis-O, and Rice could become a dry campus", and that NOD, "the largest party of the year … is essentially lost for new students" [@thresher-2025-09-03-texas-party] [@thresher-2025-08-27-culture-of-care].
- **The governing documents.** The Constitution of February 2026 does not mention NOD; the Social Vice Presidents "Plan and direct the social activities of the College including the public parties" [@constitution-2026 Art. V §1 (4)].

## What was preserved

As far as the sources say: **a Wiess public**, which the announcement expressly kept ("a 'radically different' public party of its own design") [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled]; and **an annual theme** — "much like how NOD had annual themes", MAD's officers planned to choose one each year [@thresher-2024-10-mad-announced]. The name, the dress code, the Halloween date and the Commons venue were not kept [@thresher-2024-06-05-nod-canceled] [@thresher-2025-01-mad-reviews].
