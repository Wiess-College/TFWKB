# Tabletop Theatre

Wiess Tabletop Theatre is the college's theatre company, playing in the Commons—"the same place we eat"—with the PDR as its dressing room. Its season since at least 2003 has been three or four shows: the Freshman One-Acts in September, "the only college that does" them, a fall play and a spring musical with a live orchestra. Every fourth spring the musical is *Hello, Hamlet*, a home-written spoof of Shakespeare set to re-lyriced show tunes, so that "every Wiessman has a chance to experience it once before they graduate"; the corpus has it announced for spring 2004, performed in 2012 and 2016, and sung through at a retrospective at the 60th anniversary in November 2017. The college's own histories say it was written in 1964; Rice Magazine says George Greanias '70 wrote it and played the title role in 1967; the 2003 O-Week book credited "two Wiessmen 40 years ago". Two Resident Associates are remembered through it: Stan Dodds, the physicist who built the sets in the 1990s, and Dr. Bill Wilson, who recorded the shows—"every college theatre production during his time at Rice" is in his collection at Fondren Library. The name itself is unexplained in every source.

## Variants & disputes

**Who wrote *Hello, Hamlet*, and when?** Three versions. (1) "Written by two Wiessmen 40 years ago"—the 2003 book, i.e. c.1963, two authors, unnamed [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.7 (printed 41)]. (2) "In 1964, an ambitious Wiessmen wrote and 'composed'" it—the 2005 history and the books of 2006–2017, which from 2014 name him "George Grenias" and add that he "put on the entire show in a matter of two weeks" [@wb 20050526073749 http://www.teamwiess.com/view.php?Page=history.php] [@oweek-2014 p.36]. (3) "Written by George Greanias '70, it was first performed in 1967 with Greanias in the title role"—Rice Magazine, which had access to the university's records and the man's class year [@rice-magazine-2016-wiess-traditions]. The magazine's version is the stronger: a member of the class of 1970 could not have written a Wiess musical in 1964, and the spelling "Greanias" with a class year reads like a checked fact where "Grenias" without one reads like a copied one. The O-Week books' 1964 is probably a rounding of "the '60s" that hardened into a date; the 2003 book's "two Wiessmen" has no support elsewhere. The first-performance year is 1967 until a 1960s Thresher or Campanile says otherwise.

**Which shows in 2012–14?** The 2014 book says "Last year, the fall play was The Brothers Grimm Spectaculathon, and the spring musical was Mel Brooks' The Producers" [@oweek-2014 p.42]; the college site in the same months lists *Glengarry Glen Ross* (November 2013) and *Into the Woods* (April 2014) as the fall play and spring musical [@wb 20140704224725 http://teamwiess.com/glengarry-glen-ross.html] [@wb 20140704224728 http://teamwiess.com/into-the-woods.html]. Both cannot be "last year". The site's pages carry dates and casts and are the stronger source for 2013–14; the book's paragraph was most likely carried over from the 2013 book (not in the corpus) and describes 2012–13, which fits a *Hello, Hamlet* in spring 2012 followed by *The Producers* in spring 2013. The table above says "probable" for that reason.

**Three shows or four?** "Three shows each year, two in the fall and a musical in the spring" (2006–2011 Rice section) [@oweek-2006 p.61]; "four shows each year, two plays in the fall, a musical in the spring, and a show specifically for new students" (2014) [@oweek-2014 p.63]; "generally consists of three shows", counting the One-Acts as one (2015 site) [@wb 20150313224607 http://teamwiess.com/tabletop.html]. The difference is whether the One-Acts count as one show or several; the season did not change.

**Who may act.** "Wiess is the only college that does Freshman One Acts" (2006) [@oweek-2006 p.42]; auditions "open to all Rice first-year students" (2014) [@oweek-2014 p.42]; "All other Wiess performances are open to all Rice students to direct, act, etc." (2015) [@wb 20150313224607 http://teamwiess.com/tabletop.html]. The 2014 *Into the Woods* cast, eleven of sixteen from other colleges, shows the policy in practice [@wb 20140704224728 http://teamwiess.com/into-the-woods.html]. Whether the One-Acts were Wiess-only before 2014 is not stated.

**"Theater" or "Theatre".** Both spellings are the college's own, often on the same page; this site uses the mkdocs file name's "Theatre" and quotes each source as written.

## Open questions

- **The name.** No source explains "Tabletop". The earliest attestation is the 1994 glossary's passing mentions [@handbook-1994]; whether the first productions were literally staged on Commons tables is a guess no one has sourced.
- **A full show list.** The corpus gives twelve seasons out of sixty. The Thresher's arts pages (Portal, to 2009; ricethresher.org after), Dr. Bill's recordings at Fondren (Woodson, Bill Wilson papers) and the Campanile's Wiess pages would fill the table. Rice Magazine's 1967 claim should be checked against the Thresher of spring 1967.
- **Dr. Bill's recordings.** Where exactly in Fondren is the collection, and what does it hold? The 2014 glossary is the only source [@oweek-2014 p.102].
- **The teamwiess.com Hello, Hamlet page** (2014–15) is an empty JavaScript shell in every capture [@wb 20140704224732 http://teamwiess.com/hello%2c-hamlet.html]; the wiess.wordpress.com Traditions pages of 2014 (Tabletop, Hello Hamlet) are noted in the archive index but not mirrored.
- **2008 and 2020 productions of *Hello, Hamlet***—implied by the cycle, unsourced.
- **Dodds's years.** "Third year as a Resident Associate" in the 1994 handbook and "sixth year living at Motel Wiess" on a page written in July 1998 for 1998–99 are a year apart (1992 vs 1993) [@handbook-1994] [@wb 19990222090643 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/people/associates.html]; see [Resident Associates](../people/resident-associates.md).
