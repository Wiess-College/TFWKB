# Team Wiess (the chant)

"TEAM WIESS" is two words roared slowly, over and over, from the gut. It is yelled at Beer Bike, at Powderpuff, at matriculation, at the end of every [Ubangee](ubangee.md), and, in the college's own words, "any damn time" [@riceinfo-teamwiess]. It was chosen as the college's web address in 2000 (teamwiess.com), became the name of its blog, and the root of the abbreviation TFW, which the O-Week books have glossed as "Team Wiess. Includes a little emphasis" (2003) and "Team Family (or anything else that starts with F) Wiess" (2015). The college has never agreed on when it began: its own histories say Beer Bike 1974 (2003–2008) or Beer Bike 1975 (2010 onward), the Thresher said 1984, and the man who invented the War Pig describes it as already "traditional" in the autumn of 1983. The derivation story—a "Team Xerox" commercial crossed with the "Mean Machine" chant from *The Longest Yard*—has been repeated in every Wiess history since 2003 and has never been sourced.

## The banners

Paper banners reading TEAM, FAMILY and WIESS hang in the Commons: they are in stills from the 2017 O-Week videos, hung as a descending diagonal, and the same words were painted on the Commons windows by 2019 [@wb 20170908225508 http://teamwiess.com/newstudents/videos/teammerh.png] [@wb 20190928044413 http://teamwiess.com/acapics/family.jpg]. The "Family" banner is the TFW wink made physical, the joke the glossaries have made in print since 2003: "Includes a little emphasis" (2003), "Family, right?" (2006), "Team Family (or anything else that starts with F) Wiess" (2015). In 1999 Ray Wagner declined to spell it out, "this being a family publication and all" [@oweek-2003 p.4] [@oweek-2006 p.84] [@oweek-2015 p.119] [@riceinfo-teamwiess]. "TFW" also gave its name to Wiess's Friday events, the [TFFWs](tffw.md), whose middle letters have never been settled either.

## As the college described it

!!! quote "1994 Freshman Handbook"
    "TEAM WIESS—Cheer used in support of any Wiess team, especially at Beer-Bike. (Or, really, at any other random time...)" [@handbook-1994]

!!! quote "Ray Wagner, riceinfo site, June 1999"
    "No other college chant or cheer can compare to a hundred frenzied Wiessmen roaring TEAM - WIESS! over and over. I mean, really - 'Jiba'? What the hell is *that* supposed to mean? … 'But doctor, when is it a good idea to yell Team Wiess?' Easy answer: any damn time. … For best results, don't *scream* Team Wiess! - *roar* instead. It's closer to a bellow, from the gut (from the soul, if you like). Screeching at the top of your lungs wears you out, but you can use your lungs to bellow for a good long while - thereby crushing any pathetic attempts to yell 'sucks.' NOTE: Variants are accepted, but I don't think it'd be a good idea to go into 'em here, with this being a family publication and all. TFW." [@riceinfo-teamwiess]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2003—A History of Wiess"
    "Then came the now-famous 'Team Wiess' cheer. It was derived from a Xerox commercial with the phrase 'Team Xerox' and the movie, The Longest Yard, in which football fans chanted the phrase 'Mean Machine.' The 'Team Wiess' cheer made its debut around the time of Beer-Bike in 1974. Since then, the chant has become Wiess's trademark on campus." [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.3 (printed 37)]

!!! quote "teamwiess.com history page, May 2005"
    "The 'Team Wiess' cheer made its debut around the time of Beer Bike in 1974. Since then, the deep resonating chant has become Wiess' metaphorical heartbeat and Wiess' trademark on campus." [@wb 20050526073749 http://www.teamwiess.com/view.php?Page=history.php]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2010—A History of Wiess"
    "Then came the now-famous 'Team Wiess' cheer derived from a blend of a '70s Xerox commercial with the phrase, 'Team Xerox,' the movie The Longest Yard (in which football fans chanted the phrase, 'Mean Machine') and the TeamBank chain of banks. The glorious 'Team Wiess' cheer made its debut at Beer Bike 1975 when the team celebrated its second win ever with a chant of 'Wiess Team, Wiess Team!'." [@oweek-2010 p.39]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2015—A History of Wiess"
    "The 'Team Wiess' cheer made its debut at Beer Bike in 1975 when we celebrated our glorious win." [@oweek-2015 p.11] And in the glossary: "TFW—An abbreviation of Team Family (or anything else that starts with F) Wiess." [@oweek-2015 p.119]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2024"
    "Team Family Wiess is our motto, and, at Wiess, we support and celebrate each other." [@oweek-2024 p.2]—and the glossary: "**Team Wiess.** Our one and only cheer. It is one of our oldest traditions, and an embodiment of the spirit Wiess has always been known for." [@oweek-2024 p.25]

## Variants & disputes

**When did it start?** Four answers are in the record, and they come from three kinds of source. (The books of 2019–2025 keep the 1975 version, minus "second win ever": "made its debut at Beer Bike in 1975 when we celebrated our glorious win" [@oweek-2019 p.13] [@oweek-2025 p.25].)

- **1974.** The O-Week book history written for the first class in New Wiess (2003) says "around the time of Beer-Bike in 1974" [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.3 (printed 37)]; teamwiess.com (2005) and the books of 2006, 2007 and 2008 copy it [@wb 20050526073749 http://www.teamwiess.com/view.php?Page=history.php] [@oweek-2008 part 3 p.3]. Nothing in the text says where the year came from.
- **1975.** Between the 2008 and 2010 books the sentence was rewritten: "debut at Beer Bike 1975 when the team celebrated its second win ever with a chant of 'Wiess Team, Wiess Team!'", and "the TeamBank chain of banks" joined Xerox and *The Longest Yard* in the derivation [@oweek-2010 p.39]. This is the version the current college site carries [@wiess-rice-edu about/history]. By 2015 the "second win ever" detail had been softened to "when we celebrated our glorious win" [@oweek-2015 p.11]. Whoever rewrote it had a reason—a checked Beer Bike result, or an alumnus's memory—but the book does not say. A Beer Bike result is checkable in the Thresher of April 1975.
- **1984.** The Thresher's 2002 feature on Wiess traditions gives 1984 [@thresher-2002-02-01 p.11]. Against it stands Jeff Zweig '84, who built the pig identity in 1982–84 and describes the 1983 matriculation squeal as "followed by the traditional TEAM WIESS" [@maxham-pig-document]—"traditional" already, in his first-hand account, a year before the Thresher's date. The 1984 Campanile has "TEAM WIESS!!" on the Wiess senior page [@campanile-1984], and Ann Peterson '86 places a Team Wiess Beer Bike shirt in 1983 or 1984 [@rhc 2018-02-02 friday-follies-bananas comment by Ann Peterson '86, 5 Feb 2018]. 1984 is therefore the latest the chant can have begun, not the year it did; the Thresher's date is most likely the moment the chant became loud enough to notice, when Zweig's freshmen were trained to squeal before it.
- **The 1970s, undated.** Kermit Lancaster's shirts came from "the Beer-Bike Team, which was just then calling itself 'Team Wiess'" [@lancaster-wiess-memorabilia], and Rice History Corner calls his collection "'70s era" [@rhc 2013-02-19 mixed-nuts-a-dr-baker-update-a-link-to-wiess-memorabilia-and-a-bobby-soxer-on-the-phone]. That is the one witness independent of the O-Week books, and it agrees with them on the decade—and on the detail that "Team Wiess" began as the *name of the Beer Bike team* before it was a chant.

Our reading: the 1970s origin at Beer Bike is well supported by two independent lines (the college's histories and Lancaster's shirts); 1984 is refuted as an origin by Zweig and the Campanile. Between 1974 and 1975 the record cannot choose; the 1975 version carries a checkable claim (a Beer Bike win) that the 1974 version does not.

**Where did the words come from?** Every version since 2003 gives the "Team Xerox" commercial and the "Mean Machine" chant from *The Longest Yard*; 2010 adds TeamBank. None cites a person. Lancaster's "just then calling itself" suggests the team name came first and the chant followed, which is consistent with the Xerox story (a team slogan) but does not confirm it. One check from outside the corpus: *The Longest Yard* was released in late August 1974, after that spring's Beer Bike, so a chant "derived from" the film cannot have debuted at Beer Bike 1974—unless the film came into the story later than the chant did. Either the 1974 date or the derivation is wrong; the 1975 version has no such problem.

**What the F stands for.** Wagner signed off with "TFW" in June 1999 and would not expand it, "this being a family publication and all" [@riceinfo-teamwiess]. The 2003 glossary says only "Includes a little emphasis" [@oweek-2003 p.4]; 2006 adds "Family, right?" [@oweek-2006 p.84]; 2014 writes "Team 'Family' Wiess" with the quotation marks [@oweek-2014 p.36]; 2015 settles on "Team Family (or anything else that starts with F) Wiess" [@oweek-2015 p.119]. The record is consistent: the polite expansion is a later gloss on an older, ruder one.

**Beer with the alcohol boiled out.** Not about the chant, but the 1997 Beer Bike page and the 2006 glossary disagree about what the chuggers drink; see [Beer Bike](beer-bike.md).

## Open questions

- Which Beer Bike did Wiess win in 1975, and was it the "second win ever"? The Thresher issues of April 1974 and April 1975 (Portal to Texas History) would settle whether either year's coverage mentions the chant or the team name.
- Who rewrote the history between the 2008 and 2010 O-Week books, and on what evidence? The 2009 book (parts 1, 3 and 7 are missing from the corpus) may hold the first appearance of the 1975 version.
- The Thresher feature of 1 Feb 2002 is cited from earlier research notes; its Portal ark id and the exact wording of the 1984 claim still need to be added to the bibliography.
- Kermit Lancaster's class year, and whether his Beer Bike shirts are dated—the sub-pages of his site were unreadable from the build environment.
- The current college site's history page (wiess.rice.edu/about/history) is rendered in the browser and no text capture is in the corpus; its 1975 wording is cited here on an earlier reading. Someone with a browser should save it.
- When the TEAM / FAMILY / WIESS banners were first made, and where exactly they have hung. The 2017 stills are the earliest images [@wb 20170908225508 http://teamwiess.com/newstudents/videos/teammerh.png].
- No source yet for the chant's cadence or for when the slow three-fold "TEAM WIESS" that closes an Ubangee became fixed (first described in 2003).
