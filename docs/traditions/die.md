# Die

Die is a table game played at Wiess with a single die and four cups of water. As the Historian describes it, the cups stand on the four corners of a table; a die is thrown into the air to bounce off the table, and the game runs on "hundreds of silly rules" [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05c die] [T]. It is not unique to Wiess, but at Wiess it is "ever-present". The Historian specifies water, and this page keeps to that. The written record has almost nothing on it. The only trace in the corpus is a 2025 O-Week profile of a Fellow, "an Intramural (IM) king, die demon, and" more [@oweek-2025 p.36], which uses the word as if every reader would know it.

## How it is played

Only the outline is recorded, from the Historian's notes [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05c die]:

- **The table.** One table.
- **The cups.** Four, one on each corner of the table, filled with water.
- **The throw.** A die is tossed up so that it comes down and bounces off the table.
- **The rules.** "Hundreds", and silly. None is written down in the corpus.

The notes do not say what the cups are for, or how the cups and the die interact. Anyone who plays should add the core rules, and the best of the silly ones, through the Commons.

## Variants & disputes

- **Water.** The Historian specifies water [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05c die]. It matches the college's best-known chugging contest as the books describe it: Beer Bike's chuggers have chugged water, "of course", since at least 2006 [@oweek-2006 p.51] [@oweek-2024 p.39].
- **"Not unique to Wiess."** The Historian says so [@testimony-mullen-2026-10-05c die]; the corpus has nothing on the game at other colleges, and no source says when it reached Wiess or from where.

## Open questions

- When "die" became a Wiess fixture, and whether it was played in Old Wiess. Alumni of the 2000s and 2010s would know.
- A written set of house rules, if one exists.
- Whether it has a Wiess name beyond "die".
