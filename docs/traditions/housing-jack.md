# The Housing Jack

Wiess has more members than beds. Every spring, the Housing Jack decides who gets one.

!!! abstract "TL;DR"
    - Rice guarantees you a room only for your first year. After that, each college decides who lives on campus [@rice-ga-student-life].
    - Wiess doesn't have room for everyone, so "some people get bumped off campus" [@oweek-2003 61-74 college.pdf p.4 (printed 64)].
    - Freshmen enter a random draw, the Freshman Jack. In 2024 it was a ping-pong cup game in the [Commons](../places/commons.md) [@housing-rules-2013 §II] [@thresher-2024-03-kicked-off-campus].
    - Upperclassmen then pick rooms by points, in the Room Jack. Wiess was holding a "Room Jack" by 1981 [@housing-rules-2013 §IV] [@thresher-1981-04-09-room-jack].

## Why there's a jack at all

Rice promises housing for your first year only. "The determination of available housing for sophomores, juniors, and seniors ... is made by each residential college's government" [@rice-ga-student-life].

Wiess has had fewer beds than members for a long time. The O-Week books of 2003, 2006 and 2007 put it plainly: "since we don't have room for everyone, some people get bumped off campus" [@oweek-2003 61-74 college.pdf p.4 (printed 64)] [@oweek-2006 p.65] [@oweek-2007 p.65].

## Wait, "jack"?

At Rice, "jack" means two different things.

1. **A prank** on another college. Every Wiess glossary from 2003 to 2025 says so [@oweek-2003 p.6] [@oweek-2025 p.28]. The 2016 Owlmanac: "The more clever and over-the-top (without being destructive/offensive) the better" [@owlmanac-2016 p.55].
2. **A random housing draw.** Every college has one, with names like "Housing Jack," "Eligibility Jack" and "Room Jack" [@thresher-2024-03-kicked-off-campus].

This page is about the second kind. Wiess's glossary called it "Room Draw" (2014–2019), then "Room Draw (Jack)" in 2016, then "Housing Jack" from 2021 [@oweek-2014 p.103] [@owlmanac-2016 p.56] [@oweek-2021 p.19]. The 2016 version is the most honest: "It's a little bit complicated, but you'll figure it out" [@owlmanac-2016 p.56].

## The Freshman Jack

**Who goes first.** The written rules list who gets housing, in order: people guaranteed beds (athletes, for example), then incoming students, then rising seniors, then rising juniors. Rising sophomores come sixth [@housing-rules-2013 §I]. So most of the squeeze falls on freshmen.

**The written rule.** Freshmen who want to stay on campus sign up. The housing coordinator draws names at random, one at a time. "The first name selected goes to the bottom of the waiting list for housing. The last name selected goes at the top" [@housing-rules-2013 §II]. Men and women are drawn separately [@housing-rules-2008 §II] [@housing-rules-2013 §II].

**Service hours help.** Since 2013, freshmen with at least four service hours go into the top drawing, ahead of everyone else [@housing-rules-2013 §II]. The glossary: "Necessary to enter the housing jack at the end of your first year" [@oweek-2014 p.102] [@oweek-2025 p.26]. The 2024 and 2025 books say service points "increase your odds of getting on-campus housing sophomore year" [@oweek-2024 p.34] [@oweek-2025 p.35].

**The cup game.** By 2024 the draw had become a game. The Thresher called it "The Almost-Pong Game." Cups are set out in the Commons, each with one freshman's name. The "housing apprentice" throws the first ping-pong ball, and whoever's cup it lands in is "the first freshman kicked, lowest on the waitlist and next up to bat" [@thresher-2024-03-kicked-off-campus]. That freshman throws next, and so on, "until every freshman's fate is sealed" [@thresher-2024-03-kicked-off-campus].

It's the same rule as the written one: first picked, bottom of the list. Just with ping-pong balls.

## The Room Jack

Once everyone knows who's on campus, the Room Jack decides who lives where. In 1981 the Wiess notice in the Thresher put it this way: "Say goodbye to your favorite freshmen at Room Jack Thursday night in the commons" [@thresher-1981-04-09-room-jack].

**Points.** Rising seniors get 4, juniors 3, sophomores 2, fifth-years 1. A suite's score is everyone's points added up [@housing-rules-2013 §IV].

**Squatting.** If the people living in a suite have at least 7 points between them, they can keep it. Nobody can squat on a freshman suite [@housing-rules-2013 §V].

**Presidential Privilege.** The college President, with a full suite, "trumps squatters and gets first choice of room at the college" [@housing-rules-2013 §III].

**Small suites, then everyone else.** Groups first jack for the 2-, 3- and 5-person suites, highest points first [@housing-rules-2013 §VI]. Then full suites enter the General Room Jack. Groups are called by point total, ties broken at random, and someone from each group picks a suite on the spot [@housing-rules-2013 §VII].

**Don't bail.** If you put your name on a room jack form and then withdraw after the deadline, Wiess fines you $100 [@housing-rules-2008 §VII] [@housing-rules-2013 §VII].

## Who runs it

In 2008 the Housing Committee was a Masters' Committee: one Resident Associate, the Wiess President, and three students, one from each class [@housing-rules-2008 §XI]. By 2013 it was a Resident Associate plus three students, picked in a process set by the President [@housing-rules-2013 §X].

Today the Constitution has three **Housing Representatives** (see [Representatives](../governance/representatives.md)), who "Execute all housing jacks by the end of the Spring Semester." Before the jack they choose a sophomore **Apprentice Housing Representative**, who later becomes a Housing Rep and then Senior Housing Rep [@constitution-2026 p.10]. That's the "housing apprentice" who throws the first ball [@thresher-2024-03-kicked-off-campus].

If you lose the draw, there's now a backup. Since 2026, Rice's Main Street Residence takes students "who participated in their college's housing draw but did not receive a room." Taking a spot there means giving up your place on your college's waitlist [@rice-news-2026-03-02-main-street].
