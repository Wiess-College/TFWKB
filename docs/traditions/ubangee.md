# The Ubangee

A Ubangee is a pile of Wiessmen on top of one Wiessman, grunting, that ends with three slow cries of [TEAM WIESS](team-wiess.md). The person underneath is protected by a "brace" (the 1999 word was "bracer") who kneels over them on knees and elbows while everyone else lies cross-wise on top. It is given for birthdays, for leaving, for asking "what's a Ubangee?", for yelling "Sucks!" after a Team Wiess, and, in 2003, for breaking the moment of silence at family-style dinner. The 1994 handbook called it "a unique Wiess mass celebration of life" and every glossary since has kept the phrase. Its origin and the origin of its name are unknown: the college's own history places it with the third-floor dangle among "unorthodox customs" of the early years, undated. The form has changed. The 1999 page describes a pile; the 2015 history says it is "still proudly practiced in a slightly modified form"; the 2017 O-Week book describes a circle of Wiessmen jumping and yelling around a "person of interest", with the brace sometimes also doing the tackling.

## As the college described it

!!! quote "1994 Freshman Handbook"
    "Ubangee — A unique Wiess mass celebration of life." [@handbook-1994]

!!! quote "Ray Wagner, riceinfo site, June 1999"
    "A ubangee is not a random dogpile. The bracer gets on his or her knees and elbows over the ubangeeee, and the people above them do the same thing, laying cross-wise and bracing as well as they can. Granted, this means that being the bracer can really suck, but take comfort in the fact that the person just above the bracer is the one who *really* feels it. … The proper Team Wiess response to 'hey, it's my birthday!' is *not* 'that's great.' The real thing: 'that's great - ubangee!' … Anybody who yells 'Sucks!' after a good Team Wiess! needs it bad. … Silly as it may sound, 2-man ubangees do happen and can be damn spirited. … Ceiling ubangees: generally reserved for women ubangeeees. *Carefully* pick 'em up and 'bounce' them on the ceiling." [@riceinfo-ubangee]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2003"
    "The Ubangee combines the fun of piling on top of your fellow Wiessmen with the simple joy of grunting. Every Ubangee has a victim, someone who is forced to lie helplessly under a pile of gyrating Wiessmen. (NOTE: A person known as a 'brace' crouches over the victim, protecting them from physical harm.) After a reasonable number of grunts have been grunted, the Ubangee is concluded with three triumphant cries of TEAM WIESS! … Just remember the cardinal Ubangee rule: If you've never been Ubangeed, don't tell anyone." [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.6 (printed 40)]

!!! quote "teamwiess.com Traditions page, 2014"
    "Those unfamiliar with it can't help but sit and stare. Those who know about it run with fear. Wiessmen's hearts, however, swell with pride whenever they see one. … After a reasonable number of grunts have been emitted, the Ubange is concluded with three triumphant, loud and slow cries of TEAM WIESS! … Just remember the cardinal Ubangee rule: If you've never been Ubangeed, tell everyone!" [@wb 20140627224527 http://teamwiess.com/traditions.html]

## Variants & disputes

**Pile or circle?** From 1999 to 2016 every description is of a pile: people "laying cross-wise" over a bracer (1999) [@riceinfo-ubangee], "a pile of gyrating Wiessmen" (2003–2016) [@oweek-2015 p.22]. The 2015 and 2016 histories add that the Ubangee is "still proudly practiced in a slightly modified form" without saying what was modified [@oweek-2015 p.10] [@oweek-2016 p.11]. The 2017 book says what: "a circle of jumping and yelling Wiessmen" around a "person of interest", with the brace the only one who may "tackle" [@oweek-2017 p.31]. The glossary entry, "A unique Wiess celebration of life", did not change [@oweek-2017 p.15]. The record does not say who decided this or why; the 2015 wording suggests a change already made by summer 2015.

**Victim or person of interest.** "Victim" from 2003 through the Associates' page [@wb 20170101000000 https://wiessassociates.rice.edu/wiess-101-for-associates/wiesstraditions/]; "person of interest" and "the person who is being celebrated" in 2017 [@oweek-2017 p.31].

**Don't tell anyone / tell everyone.** The 2003 book's "cardinal Ubangee rule" is "If you've never been Ubangeed, don't tell anyone" [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.6 (printed 40)] — a warning. The 2014 website's version is "tell everyone!" [@wb 20140627224527 http://teamwiess.com/traditions.html] — an invitation. Both are jokes with the same point; the website's is the later wording.

**Three cries.** "Three triumphant cries of TEAM WIESS" in 2003 [@wb 20040416083807 http://www.teamwiess.com/oweek/35-44%20wiess.pdf p.6 (printed 40)]; "three triumphant, loud and slow cries" from 2014 [@wb 20140627224527 http://teamwiess.com/traditions.html] [@oweek-2015 p.22]. Wagner's 1999 page does not mention the closing chant at all; it may have been too obvious to write down, or it may postdate him.

**Ceiling Ubangees and gender.** Only Wagner (1999) describes ceiling Ubangees, "generally reserved for women ubangeeees", and remarks that "most of the time, it's the guys who ubangee people" [@riceinfo-ubangee]. No later source repeats either; the 2003 text speaks of "Wiessmen" throughout. Treat the 1999 page as one writer's picture of the mid-1990s college, not a rule.

**"Mass".** The 1994 glossary's "mass celebration of life" lost the word "mass" in 2003 and never got it back [@handbook-1994] [@oweek-2003 p.4]; Wagner's page kept the 1994 wording as its epigraph [@riceinfo-ubangee]. Probably an edit for length.

**The 1997 text lived on.** The teamwiess.com pages of 2006–2007 are Wagner's 1999 page verbatim, down to "Evan gets ubangeed all to Hell" in the photo caption [@wb 20070801184957 http://teamwiess.com/the-ubangee.html]; the 2006 site notes it "was to merge parts of the old and much older of the site" [@wb 20060629212514 http://teamwiess.com/index.php?module=page&page=nod]. Freshmen of 2007 were reading a description written when their fellows were in grade school.

## Open questions

- **Origin: unknown.** No source in the corpus dates the first Ubangee or explains the word. The college history lists it with the dangle among customs of the early years [@wb 20050526073749 http://www.teamwiess.com/view.php?Page=history.php], but gives no decade. Alumni of the 1960s and 1970s, Woodson's Wiess records (UA 0079) and the Thresher's OCR are the places to look; a search of the Portal for "ubangee" and its spellings has not yet been logged.
- **Spelling.** "Ubangee", "ubangeed", "ubangeeee" (Wagner's spelling for the person underneath) and "Ubange" (the 2014 website) all occur. No source gives an etymology.
- **The 2015–17 change.** Who modified the form, and was it a Cabinet, Court or Magisters' decision? The 2015 book is the first to say "slightly modified form"; the corpus has no 2012 or 2013 book, so the change may be earlier.
- **Photographs.** Wagner's "Here be Ubangees!" gallery sat behind a Wiessmen-only login (`restricted/getim.html`) and was not captured [@riceinfo-ubangee]. The 1999 page's one public photograph ("Evan gets ubangeed all to Hell") is in the mirror's images.
- **Does it still happen?** The last description in the corpus is 2017 [@oweek-2017 p.31]; the Associates' page is the last outside witness. No source after 2017.
