# "What time is it?"

Ask a Wiessman the time and the answer, in the 1990s, was "It's (whatever) – *and Hanszen still sucks!*" [@riceinfo-hanszensucks]. The call-and-response is on the 1997 website's list of traditions "We'd Sure Like To See Continue" [@riceinfo-traditions-1997], and the page Ray Wagner gave it in June 1999 opens with its obituary: "This is a tradition that unfortunately seems to be dying out. In times past, many of us were so used to screaming 'And Hanszen still sucks!' anytime somebody asked us the time that we did it even when at home, where nobody got it" [@riceinfo-hanszensucks]. The page ends with a plea — "Please, let's keep this one going" — that the record does not show anyone answering. The phrase itself survived at least once more: the servery-corridor jack of c.2002–03 was repainted "Welcome to the Wiess Commons and Hanszen still sucks", a story the O-Week books told to 2014 [@oweek-2006 p.52] [@oweek-2014 p.51]. The wider rivalry is on its own page, [Hanszen](../hanszen-rivalry.md).

## As the college described it

    "This is a tradition that unfortunately seems to be dying out. In times past, many of us were so used to screaming 'And Hanszen still sucks!' anytime somebody asked us the time that we did it even when at home, where nobody got it. But it was fun anyway… Please, let's keep this one going. When somebody asks you the time, just yell: 'It's (whatever) - *and Hanszen still sucks!*'" [@riceinfo-hanszensucks]

## Variants & disputes

- **Continue, or dying.** The index said "We'd Sure Like To See Continue" in both 1997 and 1999 while the page said "dying out" [@riceinfo-traditions-1999] [@riceinfo-hanszensucks]. Wagner wrote both; the tiers were about hope, the page about fact.
- **The Backabowl railing.** "The college sucks so hard it's weakening the Backabowl railing!" [@riceinfo-hanszensucks] — the Backabowl was Old Wiess's rear field "between the singles wing and the three story wing" [@handbook-1994], the side toward Hanszen; the joke is a map.

## Open questions

- When the response began. "In times past" in 1999 [@riceinfo-hanszensucks]; nothing earlier.
- Whether it was ever revived; the books never mention it, but a two-word response would not need a glossary entry.
- Whether Hanszen had a reciprocal line; Hanszen's current traditions page does not record one [@hanszen-traditions].
