# The WiessCrack

The WiessCrack was Wiess's own publication — in 1968 a college newsletter, by 1994 "Wiess' annual humor publication, intended to mock the Thresher and improve on the Trasher. Funnier than Letterman" [@handbook-1994]. The 1999 website described how it was made: "Throughout the year (the night before it's due out) comedically-skilled Wiessmen put together articles, features and other random shit to put on paper. Due to the fact that some of the jokes are pretty damn insulting, the *'Crack* staff stays anonymous" [@riceinfo-crack]. The 1997 and 1999 traditions pages hoped it would continue [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]. It then vanishes: no O-Week glossary from 2003 to 2017 has it, though the Trasher it parodied — "The April's Fool newspaper" — stayed in the glossary until 2008 [@oweek-2008 part 7 p.7]. The oldest trace is the newest source — a 1968 cover posted by the university archivist in 2015, who noted that "Wise Crack" is the correct pronunciation of the college's name [@rhc 2015-01-23 friday-follies-wiess-crack]. No copy of any issue is in the corpus.

## As the college described it

!!! quote "1994 Freshman Handbook"
    "**WiessCrack** — Wiess' annual humor publication, intended to mock the Thresher and improve on the Trasher. Funnier than Letterman." [@handbook-1994]

!!! quote "riceinfo site, 'The Wiesscrack', 25 Jun 1999"
    "Just what is the *Wiesscrack*? Well, it's a clever, witty and satirical parody of both the 'official' Rice paper, the *Thresher*, [and the] *Thresher* staff's own pathetic attempt at funniness, the *Trasher*. (I can actually remember a time when the *Trasher* was funny, but it was a long time ago.) So Wiess has the *Wiesscrack*. Throughout the year (the night before it's due out) comedically-skilled Wiessmen put together articles, features and other random shit to put on paper." [@riceinfo-crack]

!!! quote "Rice History Corner, 23 Jan 2015"
    "What I particularly enjoy about this is that if you pronounce it as 'Wise Crack' you would be (for a refreshing change) actually pronouncing the name correctly." [@rhc 2015-01-23 friday-follies-wiess-crack]

## Variants & disputes

- **One publication or two.** The 1968 Wiess Crack is described by the archivist as a college newsletter of the 1960s "Golden Age of college newsletters" [@rhc 2015-01-23 friday-follies-wiess-crack]; the 1994 and 1999 descriptions are of an annual parody paper [@handbook-1994] [@riceinfo-crack]. The name is continuous; whether the thing was is not shown. A newsletter can turn into a parody, or a later generation can reuse a good pun.
- **The 1968 date.** Melissa Kean writes "the fall of 1968"; her image files are named "wiess-crack-2-29-68" — 29 February 1968, which existed [@rhc 2015-01-23 friday-follies-wiess-crack]. The filename is probably the issue date and the prose an approximation; the cover itself, in the Woodson, would settle it.
- **Spelling.** "WiessCrack" (1994 glossary, 1997 list), "Wiesscrack" and "'Crack" (1999 page), "Wiess Crack" (1968 cover as read by Kean). This site uses the 1994 form.
- **The comment thread.** None of the fifteen comments on the 2015 post describes the Crack; they turn to freshman beanies, NOD and *Hello, Hamlet* [@rhc 2015-01-23 friday-follies-wiess-crack]. Testimony about the paper is still wanted.

## Open questions

- Any issue of the WiessCrack, of any decade. The Woodson holds at least the 1968 one [@rhc 2015-01-23 friday-follies-wiess-crack]; the Wiess College Records [@woodson-ua0079] may hold more.
- When the last issue appeared. Live on the website in 1999 and 2001 [@riceinfo-crack]; absent from the 2003 book — a gap of two to four years in which it probably ended.
- Who Jeremy Hart was (the page's original author) and in which year he wrote it [@riceinfo-crack].
