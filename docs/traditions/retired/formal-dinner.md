# Formal Dinner, waiting and long tables

Formal Dinner and freshman waiting were Wiess's sit-down dinner traditions. From the 1950s, as the books tell it, freshmen served upperclassmen a "family-style" dinner, until it ended around 2004 [@oweek-2006 p.37] [@oweek-2006 p.42].

!!! abstract "TL;DR"
    - Family style meant big dishes on the table and everyone seated, with freshmen as waiters [@oweek-2003 wiess p.6].
    - The Thresher shows "freshman waiting" at Wiess by 1976 [@thresher-1976-11-15-freshman-waiting]. By the late 1980s Wiess was the last college still doing it five nights a week [@rhc 2013-03-15 friday-afternoon-follies-one-of-the-best-beanie-shots-of-all-times comment by George Webb, 21 Mar 2013].
    - It ended after the move to the new building and its servery [@oweek-2006 p.42].
    - One table rule survives: **cornering**, pulling up an extra chair at a table's corner [@oweek-2025 p.26].

## What it was

The O-Week histories start with the first master (now called [Magister](../../people/masters-and-magisters.md)), Dr. Roy Talmage. He "instituted mandatory Wiess blazers (complete with crests) for formal Sunday dinners, where freshmen served upperclassmen a family-style dinner" [@oweek-2006 p.37].

Weeknights were family style too. In 1991 it was in the Rules: "Freshmen shall serve at seated weekday dinners on a rotating basis." Skip your shift without a sub and you got fined [@rules-1991 §VI].

## "Freshman hazing—er, I mean freshman waiting"

In 1976 a freshman at another college teased that the "'animals' at Wiess still engage in Freshman hazing—er, I mean freshman waiting" [@thresher-1976-11-15-freshman-waiting]. A week later, a reply signed "Weiss" fired back.

The reply: freshmen "wait for approximately 8 weeks of their four years," and being served the rest of the time "is far superior to the 'hep yersef' system at the other colleges" [@thresher-1976-11-22-freshman-waiting-reply].

## How it ended

Even in 2003, in the new building, family style ran "once a week… served most often by fellow freshmen serving as waiters" [@oweek-2003 wiess p.6]. Three years later it was over:

> "Until two years ago, Wiess had a long tradition of family-style dinners every Monday through Thursday… But with our new building and beautiful servery, family style has become a thing of the past as quicker lines and more food choices made it a less-attractive option." [@oweek-2006 p.42]

An alum of the 1980s put it less politely: Wiess kept waiting "long after all other colleges had given it up, lasting until the forced advent of shared serveries destroyed any real hope of family-style dining" [@rhc 2013-03-15 friday-afternoon-follies-one-of-the-best-beanie-shots-of-all-times comment by George Webb, 21 Mar 2013]. Today's only formal dinner in the books is Associates Night, once a semester [@oweek-2025 p.26].

## Cornering

"**Corner.** 1. To pull up an extra chair at the corner of a table. Frequently occurs during meals, but never at formal occasions" (2003) [@oweek-2003 conclusions p.3]. That rule fits family style: a table sat eight, "seven other Wiessmen" and you [@oweek-2003 wiess p.6], each with a served place.

"Never" softened to "not" in 2015 [@oweek-2015 p.118]. The formal-occasion part was dropped in 2021 [@oweek-2021 p.18] [@oweek-2025 p.26]. The word survived; the reason for it didn't. Year by year: [Corner](../glossary-series.md#corner).

## Square tables

The 2003 family-style table seated eight [@oweek-2003 wiess p.6], two to a side on a square. A 2017 photo of the New Wiess [Commons](../../places/commons.md) (the dining hall) shows square tables pushed together in pairs, chairs on all four sides [@wb 20170714224839 http://teamwiess.com/newstudents/rooms/commons.jpg]. In the 1990s the tables could be moved into a U for a Viking dinner [@riceinfo-norse]. More at [The Commons and UpCo](../../places/commons.md#square-tables).
