# Retired traditions

"Retired" on this site means one thing: the record stops. A tradition is filed here when the last source that treats it as a living practice is old, and nothing later — no O-Week book, website, constitution or newspaper in the corpus — says it was still done. It is not a verdict. Some of these may be alive in a form the books stopped describing; some may have been dead before the source that "last attests" them was written. The pages say which, as far as the record allows, and a reader who knows better should put it in the Commons.

The college did this sorting itself before anyone else. The first website's traditions page of 11 August 1997 ended with a tier headed **Traditions That Sadly Seem To Be Dying Out**: Talleyboo ("when was the last time anybody at Wiess did anything *really* disgusting? I guess we're not quite as sick as I'd thought...*sigh*"), *The Princess Bride*, and No-Theme Wiess [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]. Two more were already past tense on the same site: "I'm glad 'dangling' doesn't happen anymore. Ask somebody who's already graduated, young ones" [@riceinfo-traditions-1997], and Jock Row, "one of Wiess' oldest, though now defunct, traditions" in the 1994 handbook text [@riceinfo-history]. Ray Wagner's revision of 30 December 1999 moved Unnecessary Tie Day and Norse Night down into the dying tier, promoted No-Theme Wiess out of it, and wrote on the "What time is it?" page that the chant "unfortunately seems to be dying out" [@riceinfo-traditions-1999] [@riceinfo-hanszensucks].

The site's own consolation still applies: "some things you might remember fondly from your year as a wee freshman may go the way of the dinosaurs – but don't worry; somebody'll come up with something else cool to do. Change can be good." [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]

## Named once, and no page

Three items on the 1997 lists have no page because the corpus holds nothing but the name: ***The Princess Bride*** ("Dying Out", 1997 and 1999 — presumably a screening, but no source says so) [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]; **Wiess Films** ("We'd Sure Like To See Continue", 1997 and 1999) [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]; and **Four-square in the Acabowl**, which the 1994 glossary has as "Foursquare — Good Fourplay" and the 1997 site wanted to continue [@handbook-1994] [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]. The Acatramp and four-square belong to [the Acabowl](../../places/acabowl.md).

## Open questions

- Any of these may have continued unrecorded between 2000 and 2003, the gap between the last riceinfo captures and the first O-Week book in the corpus.
- The 1994 Freshman Handbook itself, and any earlier handbooks, would show which of these were already being described to freshmen before 1997 [@handbook-1994].
- Nothing here has been checked against the Thresher's archive after 1999 [@thresher-web]; a search for each name is a cheap next step.
