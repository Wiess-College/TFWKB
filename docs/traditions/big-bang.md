# Big Bang

It's coming.

??? danger "Spoilers ahead — opening this will ruin all the fun"
    **Summary.** Big Bang is the event the college throws for its new students once they have survived their first round of exams—"a variety of games, dancing and, of course, goldenrod T-shirts" in the O-Week books' words [@oweek-2003 35-44 wiess.pdf p.8]. It is in the record from 1984, when a Thresher personal told visitors from Brown that "every decent Wiess man was at the Pub. Or didn't you know it was Big Bang Nite?" [@thresher-1984-10-05-big-bang], and in 1986 the Thresher reported "the third annual Big Bang Night" after the first Physics 101 test [@thresher-1986-09-19-big-bang]. In 1997 the first website listed it with a sigh—"Big Bang (is that supposed article of the Wiess Constitution gone now? *sigh...*)" [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]; it is in every O-Week glossary from 2003 to 2014, and in the 2026 Constitution, which makes it the Internal Vice President's job "to be planned with the Head Fellow(s)" [@constitution-2026 p.6]. No constitution between 1993 and 2017 mentions it, so the "supposed article" of 1997 was either lost before 1993 or never existed; the 2026 text is the first to write it in. The books always kept the details back ("The exact details are a mystery…" [@oweek-2014 p.43]). In 2008 and 2009 the college website paired it with [DOME](dome.md), under the menu heading "DOME & Big Bang" [@wb 20080625214640 http://teamwiess.com/index.php?r=domebigb].

    **As the college described it.**

    > **riceinfo site, 1997:** "Big Bang (is that supposed article of the Wiess Constitution gone now? *sigh...*)" [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]

    > **O-Week Book 2003:** "Wiess throws a party for the freshmen class as a congratulations for surviving the first wave of exams and assignments. It's an opportunity to relax with a variety of games, dancing and, of course, goldenrod T-shirts." [@oweek-2003 35-44 wiess.pdf p.8] (unchanged through 2011 [@oweek-2011 p.45], and on the website 2009–10 [@teamwiess-activities-2009])

    > **O-Week Book 2014:** "Every year, Wiess throws a party for the first-year class as congratulations for surviving the first wave of exams and assignments. The exact details are a mystery… but rest assured that you will get to relax with a variety of games, dancing, and of course, goldenrod T-shirts." [@oweek-2014 p.43]

    > **O-Week Book 2017:** "Internal VP: … coordinating Summit, Wiess Shark Tank, Pumpkin Caroling, and Big Bang, whatever that is." [@oweek-2017 p.25]

    > **Constitution, 23 Feb 2026:** "Plan Wiess College Summit and Big Bang. i. Big Bang is to be planned with the Head Fellow(s)." [@constitution-2026 p.6]

    The glossary line was "Party held for the freshmen after they ace their first round of exams" 2003–2011 [@oweek-2003 p.3] [@oweek-2011 p.92], "Event held for the new students after they ace their first round of exams" in 2014 [@oweek-2014 p.102], and absent 2015–2017.

    **Variants & disputes.**

    - *The constitutional article.* Cunningham in 1997 believed Big Bang had once been written into the Constitution [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]. The 1993 Constitution he himself posted does not contain it [@constitution-1993], nor do the 2007, 2013, 2016 or 2017 texts or the 2010 Bylaws [@gov-repo]. Either the article belonged to a pre-1993 constitution—the 1993 text says it replaced one with "ten amendments"—or the belief was a campus legend. The Woodson records are the place to settle it [@woodson-ua0079]. In 2026 the Constitution does name Big Bang, for the first time in the surviving lineage [@constitution-2026 p.6].
    - *Party or event.* The books describe a party with "games, dancing" and T-shirts [@oweek-2003 35-44 wiess.pdf p.8]; from 2014 they add "The exact details are a mystery" and change "Party" to "Event" [@oweek-2014 p.43] [@oweek-2014 p.102]. The 1986 Thresher ties it to the first Physics 101 test [@thresher-1986-09-19-big-bang]. The 2014 change of wording also tracks Rice's move from "freshmen" to "new students".
    - *Did it lapse?* Big Bang vanishes from the 2015 and 2016 books entirely and returns in 2017 as something the Internal VP coordinates "whatever that is" [@oweek-2017 p.25]. The joke may mean the event had become obscure, or only that the writer was being funny. The 2026 Constitution treats it as live [@constitution-2026 p.6].
    - *DOME and Big Bang.* The 2008 menu put them under one heading [@wb 20080625214640 http://teamwiess.com/index.php?r=domebigb] and the 2009 page printed them one after the other [@teamwiess-activities-2009]. Neither says why. They are both early-fall events for (or about) the new students, which is the likeliest reason for the pairing. See [DOME](dome.md).

    **Open questions.**

    - Whether a pre-1993 constitution had a "Big Bang" article, as the 1997 site believed [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]—Woodson UA 0079 [@woodson-ua0079].
    - When Big Bang began. The 1986 "third annual" points to 1984, the year of the first trace [@thresher-1984-10-05-big-bang] [@thresher-1986-09-19-big-bang]; the Thresher of 1983–85 should be checked for an announcement.
    - What Big Bang was in 2024, 2025 and 2026, year by year; the coordinators' events list (see [O-Week](o-week.md)) may say. The O-Week books of those years do not [@oweek-2024 p.37] [@oweek-2025 p.38].
    - Whether Big Bang was held in 2014–2016, when the books stopped describing it.
    - What the empty 2008 "DOME & Big Bang" page was meant to say; the 2007–08 Cabinet minutes archive was never captured.
