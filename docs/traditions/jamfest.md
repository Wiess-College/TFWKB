# JamFest

JamFest was Wiess's spring music festival: a free day of live bands and food in the [Acabowl](../places/acabowl.md), usually on a Friday in mid-April during Owl Weekend, when admitted students visit [@oweek-2003 p.4] [@portal metapth246567 p.15]. It began as a talent show in the [Commons](../places/commons.md) by 1987, moved outdoors by 1993, and from 2010 shared its place with [Wiess Day](wiess-day.md), until the 2014 book said it had "come and gone" [@thresher-1987-03-20-jamfest] [@portal metapth245840 p.20] [@oweek-2010 p.91] [@oweek-2014 p.37].

!!! abstract "TL;DR"
    - Wiess, Rice and Houston bands played, and later touring headliners too [@wb 19990220115332 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/traditions/jamfest.html] [@teamwiess-jamfest-site].
    - At its biggest (2005–09) it ran ten or twelve hours, with 12 to 14 acts [@teamwiess-jamfest-site] [@oweek-2006 p.23] [@portal metapth443077 p.23].
    - Headliners included Bowling for Soup (2002, 2003), The Junior Varsity (2005), Makeshifte (2006) and General Noise (2007) [@oweek-2003 35-44 wiess.pdf p.9] [@portal metapth398462 p.9] [@wb 20070206202205 http://www.teamwiess.com/Jamfest/main.htm] [@portal metapth443062 p.9] [@portal metapth443077 p.23].
    - By 2010 it was down to three hours and three student bands; its successor is [Wiess Day](wiess-day.md) [@portal metapth398481 p.15] [@oweek-2014 p.37].

## How it worked

"Spend the whole day lying on a towel, eating hamburgers and hanging with your friends," the 2003 book said [@oweek-2003 35-44 wiess.pdf p.9]. It was free, and from 1993 it usually ran from the afternoon deep into the night [@portal metapth245840 p.20] [@portal metapth443074 p.31].

**The music.** Student bands from around Rice played, plus Houston bands and, in the 2000s, a touring headliner [@portal metapth246676 p.19] [@wb 20070206202205 http://www.teamwiess.com/Jamfest/main.htm]. In 2005 there were two stages [@wb 20070206202123 http://www.teamwiess.com/Jamfest/playlist.htm]. By 2008, "Bands from around Rice audition to get a timeslot" (so far only the college website says this) [@wb 20090917020034 http://teamwiess.com/activities.php].

**The sound.** Dr. Bill Wilson, a longtime Wiess RA , ran "pretty much all the 'tech'" for Wiess events, JamFest included, and lent students his audio gear [@portal metapth443181 p.1] [@portal metapth443018 p.6]. In 2006 a Fellow "ran the soundboard for all 10 hours" [@oweek-2006 p.23]. In 2007 the [Dr. Bill Wilson Student Initiative Grant](https://drbillgrant.rice.edu/recipient-history/) lists "Wiess JamFest Audio Equipment" among its awards (one source so far). More on him at [Resident Associates](../people/resident-associates.md).

**The extras.** Over the years there was free pizza and drinks (1993), tie-dyeing (1997), barbecue and beer with a canned-food drive and $2 T-shirts (1998), face painting and henna (2005), and an iPod raffle for T-shirt buyers (2007) [@portal metapth245840 p.20] [@portal metapth246567 p.15] [@portal metapth246621 p.19] [@wb 20070206202205 http://www.teamwiess.com/Jamfest/main.htm] [@portal metapth443077 p.23]. In 2009 there was a Rock Band set, a moonbounce and sumo suits [@portal metapth443096 p.19]. In 1997–98 the Princeton Review counted JamFest among the groups it had worked with, the first outside sponsor we've found (one ad, printed twice) [@portal metapth246622 p.9] [@portal metapth246624 p.14].

## From talent show to festival

In March 1987 the Thresher announced "JamFest, an open musical talent show, in the Commons" [@thresher-1987-03-20-jamfest]. It was a talent show again in 1988 [@portal metapth245688 p.20]. A 2006 Thresher feature called it the 38th JamFest, which would put the first in 1968; the earliest we've found so far is 1987 [@portal metapth443062 p.9] [@thresher-1987-03-20-jamfest].

In 1992 the ads said "JAMFEST RETURNS", now asking for bands [@portal metapth245802 p.24] [@portal metapth245808 p.20]. In 1993 it moved to the Acabowl in mid-April, with "TG" and free pizza [@portal metapth245840 p.20]. By 1997 it was "an annual party/concert held at Wiess every spring, featuring lots of Wiess/Rice/Houston bands" [@wb 19990220115332 http://riceinfo.rice.edu/projects/colleges/wiess/traditions/jamfest.html]. That year the Thresher listed Wiess's two big parties as "NOD (October), Jamfest (March)" [@portal metapth246561 p.5].

## The big years

The 2003 book tells it this way: "What started as a handful of makeshift bands playing in the Acabowl has become an all-day festival" [@oweek-2003 35-44 wiess.pdf p.9]. In 2002 and 2003 the headliner was the Grammy-nominated Bowling for Soup [@oweek-2003 35-44 wiess.pdf p.9] [@portal metapth398462 p.9].

Some years had a theme: "Acapalooza" in 1997, the Cajun "Jambalayafest" in 2001, and "JAMaica me crazy FEST mon!" in 2002 [@portal metapth246567 p.15] [@portal metapth443074 p.31] [@portal metapth443185 p.19].

JamFest 2005 ran from 4 p.m. to 2 a.m., with fourteen acts on two stages, plus face painting and henna [@wb 20070206202205 http://www.teamwiess.com/Jamfest/main.htm] [@wb 20070206202123 http://www.teamwiess.com/Jamfest/playlist.htm]. The 2007 show started at 2 p.m. [@wb 20070709183135 http://teamwiess.com/Jamfest/main.htm]. It used to be held on Owl Weekend, when admitted students visit [@oweek-2003 p.4].

Not everyone was impressed. A 2004 op-ed said "Even large events like Wiess Jamfest are poorly attended" [@portal metapth398440 p.3]. Two years later a Thresher editorial pointed to "the popularity of Wiess' annual Jamfest" [@portal metapth443047 p.2].

## Fade out

Wiess Day began in spring 2008 as a separate party [@oweek-2008 part 3 p.8]. In 2010 JamFest shrank to three hours and three Rice bands [@portal metapth398481 p.15]. That year the glossary lumped them together as "Jamfest/Wiess Day" [@oweek-2010 p.91]. 2011 is the latest book we've found that describes a JamFest [@oweek-2011 p.46], and the 2011 show added comedy [@portal metapth398487 p.15]. We don't hold the 2012 and 2013 books yet.

The 2014–15 budget still had its own JamFest line, next to Wiess Day [@budget-2014-15 p.1]. We haven't found yet whether a JamFest was held that year.
