# Traditions

The college sorted its own traditions before anyone else did. In August 1997 David Cunningham's traditions page on the first Wiess website put them in three tiers. **Traditions Older Than Time** ("These traditions need never die!"): TEAM WIESS, the Ubangee, "the mighty War Pig", Beer Bike, Pumpkin Caroling, the Freshman Jack and College Night. **Traditions We'd Sure Like To See Continue**: Wiess Films, the Battle Sows, four-square in the Acabowl, the Freshman Weenie Roast, Unnecessary Tie Day, "What time is it?", Big Bang, the WiessCrack, JamFest, FilmFest, Norse Night and "the utter and complete obliteration of 13". **Traditions That Sadly Seem To Be Dying Out**: Talleyboo, *The Princess Bride* and No-Theme Wiess [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]. The page's own warning still holds: "Traditions come and go, like anything else, and some things you might remember fondly from your year as a wee freshman may go the way of the dinosaurs - but don't worry; somebody'll come up with something else cool to do. Change can be good." [@riceinfo-traditions-1997]

Ray Wagner's December 1999 revision kept the tiers but moved things between them: Unnecessary Tie Day and Norse Night dropped to "Dying Out"; No-Theme Wiess was promoted out of it [@riceinfo-traditions-1999] (four-square stayed in the middle tier both years). The tables below repeat the exercise for 2026, using the oldest attestation in our corpus and the latest source that says anything about a tradition's state. "Unknown" means the record goes quiet, not that the tradition did.

## Variants & disputes

- **When did Wiess Day begin?** The 2015 college website: "In the spring of 2008, a new tradition began: Wiess Day!" [@wb 20150313224552 http://teamwiess.com/traditions.html]. The O-Week books call the same spring party in the Acabowl JamFest from 2003 [@oweek-2003 p.4], "Jamfest/Wiess Day" in 2010–11 [@oweek-2010 p.91] [@oweek-2011 p.93], and Wiess Day alone from 2014 [@oweek-2014 p.103]. The books are the stronger evidence: a rebranding remembered as a founding.
- **Which tier?** Cunningham (1997) and Wagner (1999) disagree on three traditions, as above [@riceinfo-traditions-1997] [@riceinfo-traditions-1999]. Each is one writer's opinion in one year; we record both.
- **Is the War Pig the mascot?** "The Wiess mascot" in 1994 and 2003 [@handbook-1994] [@oweek-2003 p.4]; "Former Wiess mascot" in every book 2006–2011 [@oweek-2006 p.84] [@oweek-2011 p.93]; "Wiess mascot, embodied by the giant wooden pig built by the Class of 2012" from 2014 [@oweek-2014 p.103]. See [The War Pig](warpig.md).
- **Hanszen.** "A lesser college" from 2003 to 2011, with an Acaterrace "that we unfortunately have to share with Hanszen" [@oweek-2003 p.3] [@oweek-2011 p.92]; from 2014 the terrace is one "that we share with Hanszen" [@oweek-2014 p.102]. Hanszen tells the rivalry from its side [@hanszen-traditions].
- **The current college site.** wiess.rice.edu renders its Traditions page in the browser only and no capture of it is in the corpus, so the status column rests on the 2026 Constitution, the Thresher and the image names the site publishes [@wiess-rice-edu asset-manifest.json]. Someone with a browser should read the page and record what it says.

## How the glossary series works

Every O-Week book from 2003 ends with a glossary ("Wiess Speak", then "Rice Speak"), and the 1994 Freshman Handbook's glossary survives on the 1997–2000 website. Each is extracted into `sources/glossaries/<year>.tsv`, one row per entry, definition verbatim, cited to book and PDF page; `tools/build_glossary_series.py` lines the definitions up term by term. The result, [How we described ourselves, by year](glossary-series.md), is the quickest way to watch a tradition change in the college's own words: the War Pig losing and regaining its mascot status, Sue becoming "Ex-College Coordinator", Club 13 becoming Baker 13, "unfortunately" dropping out of the sentence about Hanszen.
