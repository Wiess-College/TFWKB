# Off the books

Off the books is the unofficial side of Wiess: privates, Dis-O, Toke, jacks and a few more things students keep doing even though the college doesn't run them. Some the college would rather you didn't. But they come back year after year, and Wiess knows itself by them.

!!! abstract "TL;DR"
    - Some are in writing. Some are only remembered. Each entry says which.
    - Nothing here is advice or instructions. It's history.

!!! note "Know more?"
    Where an entry says "no written source yet," we'd love your help. [Add your story](../about/contribute.md).

## Privates vs. publics

A **public** is a themed party thrown by a residential college and open to all undergrads [@oweek-2015 p.122] [@oweek-2021 p.22]. A **private** is "thrown by a group of people, rather than the whole college," with rules "outlined by the Alcohol Policy" [@oweek-2015 p.122]. The Owlmanac adds: "Known as a 'private.'" [@owlmanac-2016 p.56]

In 2003 the O-Week book said it stops being private once it "spills into public areas, involves excessive noise, public advertisement" [@oweek-2003 college p.10]. Rice tightened the rules for privates in 1999 and again in 2013. Hosts now register with their college's chief justice at least 24 hours ahead and stay at the party [@thresher-1999-04-23-alcohol-policy] [@thresher-2013-04-18-alcohol-policy].

*In writing:* glossaries 2015–2021, O-Week books 2003 and 2015, policies of 1999 and 2013.

## Pregames and room parties

Room parties are privates by another name. The 2015 book says privates "range from multiple people packed into one dorm room to a laidback gathering of just a few friends" [@oweek-2015 p.105]. Register your privates!

Pregames: no written source yet (one may never exist).

## Dis-O

Short for "Dis-Orientation," it lands right after O-Week, at the start of the fall [@thresher-2013-04-18-alcohol-policy] [@teamwiess-current-students-2014]. The university doesn't run it. Rice has long run a Welcome Back Festival the same day "to provide alternate activities to daytime drinking" [@thresher-2012-09-letters-rpc-dis-o].

Rice has pushed back on it. In 2013 it banned hard alcohol on "major events like Dis-Orientation and Beer Bike" [@thresher-2013-04-18-alcohol-policy]. In 2025 Dis-O's alcohol transports were "comparable to the seven NOD transports" of 2023 [@thresher-2025-08-26-dis-o-transports], and the Thresher warned, "another night like Dis-O, and Rice could become a dry campus" [@thresher-2025-08-27-culture-of-care].

The Wiess trace: in 2014 the college website ran a "Dis-O 2014" countdown clock [@teamwiess-current-students-2014]. Who hosts Dis-O, and when it began, aren't in the sources we've found yet.

## Toke

For context only: Rice's smoking policy (Policy 839, 2010) bans smoking in student housing and near doors and windows, and says "Colleges may designate smoking areas" [@thresher-2010-09-smoking-policy]. Since 2012, smoking on campus has been limited to 13 designated areas [@thresher-2012-08-30-smoking-policy].

## Jacks

A jack is "A prank pulled on another college" [@oweek-2003 p.6] [@oweek-2025 p.28]. The Owlmanac's rule of thumb: "The more clever and over-the-top (without being destructive/offensive) the better" [@owlmanac-2016 p.55]. Piggy Week, the week before Beer Bike, is "filled with college activities, alumni, and jacks" [@oweek-2021 p.19].

The famous one: on 12 April 1988, students turned Willy's statue around to face the library. Eight of the eleven later named were Wiess '88 [@thresher-2008-04-17-willy-revolutionaries] [@rice-news-2011-11-04-traditions]. Wiess also once repainted Hanszen's welcome corridor (see [Hanszen](hanszen-rivalry.md)).

Jacks aren't a free-for-all. By 2008 a jack needed approval from a college's jacks committee. After an unapproved jack injured students that year, the dean set limits: "you can't violate the law, you can't violate the code of conduct and you can't endanger people's lives or well-being" [@thresher-2008-03-20-unauthorized-jack].
