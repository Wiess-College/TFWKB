# Battle Sows & Powderpuff

The Battle Sows are Wiess's women's flag-football team in the Rice intramural league the campus calls Powderpuff, played in the fall. The name was proposed by Jeff Zweig '84 in the fall of 1983, the first semester the college was coed, and is in print in the Thresher's intramural standings by 30 September 1983 [@maxham-pig-document] [@portal metapth245539 p.15]. It is the first of the college's pig names to reach print — a semester before "Warpig" appeared in the 1984 Campanile [@campanile-1984 p.371] — and older than the first pig balloon, which was built for a Powderpuff game [@maxham-pig-document]; the balloon itself was called "the Battle Sow" in 1987 and 2002 [@campanile-1987 p.320] [@thresher-2002-04-05 p.6]. The team won championships in 1995, 1998, 1999, 2001 and 2002 by the college's own count and claimed by 2014 to have "won the championship more than any other college" [@campanile-1999 p.239] [@riceinfo-battlesows] [@oweek-2003 p.3] [@oweek-2014 p.41]. The games, with their sideline barbecues and half-time shows, have been described to every freshman class since 2003 in nearly the same words, and the current college site published a `powderpuff2023` photograph [@wiess-rice-edu asset-manifest.json]. Hanszen, the neighbour and rival, roasts a pig on its sideline when the two colleges meet [@hanszen-traditions].

## As the college described it

!!! quote "1994 Freshman Handbook"
    "**Battle Sows** — Affectionate name for Wiess' dauntless Powder-Puff football team, and most other women's college sports teams." [@handbook-1994]

!!! quote "riceinfo site, 30 Dec 1999"
    "The Wiess Battle Sows: 1999 Powder Puff Champions. Here's some pictures of the 1999 Wiess Battle Sows." [@riceinfo-battlesows]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2003"
    "A huge Wiess activity in the fall is intramural women's flag football, affectionately known as powderpuff. Each of the colleges has a team, but as with everything on campus, Wiess is far above the rest. The back-to-back defending champions — known as the Battlesows — are always looking for more ladies to play — no experience necessary… The weekly games turn into an event for the entire college, complete with raucus cheering, wild halftime shows and the semi-regular BBQ." [@oweek-2003 35-44 wiess.pdf p.7]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2006"
    "While little girls may not daydream about one day growing up to be Battlesows, playing on the Wiess women's intramural flag football team is certainly the stuff of dreams… Powderpuff has become a great source of pride for Wiess in the last decade or so, and alums in the area will often make appearances at the games: women telling of their great touchdown of '95, and men speaking fondly of the awe on the sidelines as she threw that 40-yard touchdown-scoring pass." [@oweek-2006 p.41]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2011"
    "Wiess has won the championship many times in the past, and although we haven't made it to the finals recently, we still dominate on the field." [@oweek-2011 p.43]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2015"
    "Known across campus as Powderpuff, the women's flag football league is one of the most hotly contested sports leagues on campus. Wiess has won the championship more than any other college and we have the potential to add to that total this year." [@oweek-2015 p.22] (unchanged in 2016 and 2017 [@oweek-2016 p.30] [@oweek-2017 p.31])

The glossary entry itself hardly moved: "Affectionate name for the back-to-back defending champion Wiess powderpuff football team" from 2003 to 2010 [@oweek-2003 p.3] [@oweek-2010 p.90] — the "back-to-back" surviving eight years after the titles it referred to — then simply "Affectionate name for the Wiess powderpuff football team" from 2011 to 2017 [@oweek-2011 p.92] [@oweek-2017 p.14].

## Variants & disputes

- **How many championships.** The record gives titles in 1995 and 1998 [@campanile-1999 p.239], 1999 [@riceinfo-battlesows], and 2001 and 2002 ("back-to-back defending champions" in a book written in summer 2003) [@oweek-2003 35-44 wiess.pdf p.7] — five between 1995 and 2002. The 2006 book then says "four times" in "the past six years" [@oweek-2006 p.41], and the 2007 and 2008 books "eight times" in "the past 15 years" [@oweek-2007 p.41] [@oweek-2008 part 3 p.6]. The two counts use different windows and were written by different coordinators a year apart; neither can be checked against a season-by-season list, which we do not have. From 2010 the books stop counting ("Past champions… hasn't made it to the finals lately") [@oweek-2010 p.42] and from 2014 claim only "more than any other college" [@oweek-2014 p.41]. Treat every number as the college's own tally, not a league record.
- **One word or two.** "Battle Sows" in 1983, 1984, 1994 and 1999 [@portal metapth245539 p.15] [@campanile-1984 p.371] [@handbook-1994] [@riceinfo-battlesows]; "Battlesows" in every O-Week glossary from 2003 [@oweek-2003 p.3] and in the 2007 book's officer blurbs ("the Wiess Battlesows", "the Battlesow Powderpuff team") [@oweek-2007 p.19]. The 2009 book has "Battle Sows" again [@oweek-2009 part 2 p.19]. Both spellings are the college's.
- **Whose name it is.** The 1994 glossary gives it to the Powder-Puff team "and most other women's college sports teams" [@handbook-1994]; Zweig says he proposed it for "the women's sports teams" generally [@maxham-pig-document]. Every book from 2003 attaches it to the powderpuff team alone.
- **The sow that flies.** "Battle Sow" was also used for the pig balloon — "Pre-flight for the Battle Sow" (1987) [@campanile-1987 p.320] and "Team Wiess's battle sow flying high in the sky" (2002) [@thresher-2002-04-05 p.6] — while the college's own glossary called it the War Pig throughout [@handbook-1994]. The two names belong to the same identity, coined in the same year; see [The War Pig](warpig.md).
- **Date of the schedule page.** The brief for this page placed the teamwiess.com Powderpuff schedule in 2005; the only captures in the corpus are 22 Oct and 7 Dec 2004 and the page is headed "Full Rice Powderpuff Schedule 2004" [@wb 20041207030444 http://www.teamwiess.com/view.php?Page=ppuff.php].

## Open questions

- The exact wording of the 30 Sep and 7 Oct 1983 Thresher standings (which sport, which division) — the Portal pages were not reachable from the build environment; someone with a browser should transcribe them into `thresher-1983-09-30` in the bibliography.
- A season-by-season championship list, 1983–present. The Thresher's intramural columns and the Campanile are the places to look; the counts in the 2006–2008 books cannot be reconciled without one.
- Whether the first pig balloon's Powderpuff game (1986) is in the Thresher; Maxham's account is the only source [@maxham-pig-document].
- Hanszen's traditions page was read live on 2026-10-04 and is not captured in the corpus; a Wayback capture should be made and the date of its roast-pig sentence established.
- Whether "Battle Sows" is still the team's name in 2026: the current site's image is named only `powderpuff2023` [@wiess-rice-edu asset-manifest.json].
