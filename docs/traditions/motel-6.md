# Motel 6 / Motel Wiess

Wiess has been called a motel for as long as it has been described at all, first by its own students and then by its neighbours, and it has taken the name for itself. The 1972 Freshman Handbook judged that the building's plan put "Dining facilities, lounge, and office… all centrally located in the best motel tradition. In fact, the only missing elements are the usual gaudy neon signs and acres of parking lots" [@handbook-1972]. Hanszen called the architecture "Early Motel 6" in the early 1980s and flew a banner reading "Motel Wiess: Hourly Rates Available" over a football game in the late 1980s; Wiess answered with a Night of Decadence called "Motel Wiess" [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by James Medford, 5 Dec 2012] [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by George Webb, 5 Dec 2012]. By 1998 the college website had a Resident Associate "living at Motel Wiess" [@riceinfo-associates], and since 2006 every O-Week history has credited "the unique 'Motel 6' design with outdoor hallways and wrap-around balconies" with the college's reputation [@oweek-2006 p.37] [@oweek-2025 p.24]. The name outlived the building it described.

## As the college described it

!!! quote "1972 Freshman Handbook"
    "In plan, the only suitable comparison is to mid-twentieth century design… Dining facilities, lounge, and office are all centrally located in the best motel tradition. In fact, the only missing elements are the usual gaudy neon signs and acres of parking lots." [@handbook-1972]

!!! quote "riceinfo Associates page, 1998"
    "Stan Dodds… this will be his sixth year living at Motel Wiess. Count on him to be the most reliable of stage builders for Tabletop Theater." [@riceinfo-associates]

!!! quote "Colin Delany '91, 2002"
    "Wiess dormitory had more in common with contemporary motel design than it did with the rest of the Rice campus." [@delany-about-wiess-2002]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2006"
    "Though extra wings, a second Commons area, and a basement were added later, the unique 'Motel 6' design with outdoor hallways and wrap-around balconies brought Wiess instant notoriety for its great social atmosphere." [@oweek-2006 p.37]

!!! quote "O-Week Book 2014"
    "At Wiess, architecture truly is destiny as the unique 'Motel 6' design with outdoor hallways and wrap-around balconies brought instant notoriety for its rowdy social atmosphere." [@oweek-2014 p.36]

!!! quote "Rice History Corner, 2016"
    "As a Weiss man myself, [I] thought is was functional and practical. Kinda Motel Sexy." [@rhc 2016-05-24 hanging-out-at-wiess comment by Buddy Chuoke '75, 25 May 2016]

## Variants & disputes

- **Insult or boast?** Both, from the start. The 1972 handbook mocks its own building [@handbook-1972]; Hanszen used the name as an insult in the 1980s, and Wiess turned it into a NOD theme the same year [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by George Webb, 5 Dec 2012]. The O-Week books since 2006 use it as a boast. The building made the college's "notoriety" [@oweek-2006 p.37].
- **Motel Wiess or Motel 6.** "Motel Wiess" is Wiess's own form, in the late-1980s NOD and the 1998 website [@riceinfo-associates]; "Motel 6", the chain, is the outsiders' form, Hanszen's "Early Motel 6" [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by James Medford, 5 Dec 2012], and became the books' form from 2006. The themes graphic's "Wiess Palms Motel 6" combines them (see [Night of Decadence](night-of-decadence.md#themes-year-by-year)).
- **Which NOD, and which year?** The graphic puts "Sanitized for your protection" with a 1977 "Wiess Palms Motel 6" and lists "Motel Wiess" for 1988; George Webb '88 gives the tagline to the late-1980s "Motel Wiess" [@rhc 2012-12-04 wiess-hall-construction-1949 comment by George Webb, 5 Dec 2012]. The dispute is set out on [Night of Decadence](night-of-decadence.md#themes-year-by-year). Both may be right: "Sanitized for your protection", the paper band on motel toilet seats, is the obvious tagline for any motel theme.
- **The name and the new building.** The O-Week books apply "Motel 6" to Old Wiess, the 1949 design [@oweek-2025 p.24]. New Wiess was built to keep the outdoor corridors and balconies on purpose [@riceinfo-news-2000-lundin], so the name carried over with the layout.
